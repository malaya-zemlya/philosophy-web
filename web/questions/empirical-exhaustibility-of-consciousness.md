The question asks whether everything there is to know about consciousness can in principle be found
out by experiment and the theories built on it, or whether some questions about consciousness are
philosophical in a way that empirical work cannot settle. It was the title of a discussion in the
Russian *Philosophy Journal* opened by Igor Mikhailov ([Mikhailov, 2024](../sources/mikhailov-2024.md)).

The question divides into several sub-questions:
- What is the object of study: conscious states, their contents, or both? And are access
  consciousness and phenomenal consciousness one phenomenon or two?
- Can conscious functions be given a complete algorithmic description?
- What criteria make an empirical theory of consciousness relevant and testable?
- Which questions about consciousness are metaphysical rather than empirical, and can they be answered
  without experiment?

An answer bears on the division of labour between the philosophy of mind and the science of
consciousness, and on whether the [hard problem](hard-problem.md) is a scientific problem at
all.

### In plain terms

Scientists study consciousness with brain scans and experiments. The question is whether that kind of
study could, at least in principle, tell us everything about consciousness, or whether some questions,
such as what consciousness ultimately is, have to be answered by careful reasoning instead.
