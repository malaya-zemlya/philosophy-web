If there is no single scale on which minds can be ranked from less to more intelligent, then the
phrase at the heart of the [physical-possibility claim](../claims/superintelligence-physically-possible.md) —
a system that "greatly exceeds the best human cognitive performance across virtually all domains" —
has no determinate subject matter. The multidimensionality objection presses this: it does not
dispute the physics of what silicon can compute, but the *coherence* of what superintelligence is
supposed to be. Before one can ask whether a superhuman intellect is possible, the notion of "more
intelligent than any human, überhaupt" has to pick out something, and on the bundle view of
[intelligence](../concepts/intelligence.md) it does not.

The inference runs from the bundle thesis to a charge of ill-formedness.

1. Intelligence is not a single scalar quantity but a heterogeneous possibility-space of distinct,
   partly incommensurable capacities — perception, memory, planning, language, social insight, motor
   control, and more ([intelligence-not-one-dimensional](../claims/intelligence-not-one-dimensional.md), [kelly-2017](../sources/kelly-2017.md)).
2. If cognition is such a space, there is no total ordering of minds: for many pairs of minds neither
   is "more intelligent overall," only differently intelligent, occupying different regions of the
   space. Ranking them by one number is like ranking animals by a single "size."
3. "Superintelligence," as usually stated, presupposes exactly such a total ordering — it names a
   point *above* the entire human range on a single across-the-board axis. With no such axis, "smarter
   than every human at everything, on the whole" is undefined.
4. Therefore the physical-possibility claim, insofar as it asserts that *this* — an all-domains
   superior intellect — is compatible with physics, lacks determinate content: there is no
   well-defined state of affairs for physics to permit or forbid. The objection attacks the claim's
   coherence, not its physics.

### Weakest premise

The point of attack against the objection itself is the passage from premise 3. Bostrom's own
definition does not obviously posit a single scalar: it quantifies over domains *one by one*, calling
superintelligent a system that exceeds the best humans "in virtually all domains of interest"
([bostrom-2014](../sources/bostrom-2014.md)). Read that way, "superintelligence" is a conjunction of separate,
domain-relative comparisons — better at physics, better at strategy, better at persuasion, and so on
across the list — each of which can be well-defined even if no common scale sums them into one
number. The bundle thesis grants that a system can exceed humans on any given capacity, and even on
many at once ([intelligence-not-one-dimensional](../claims/intelligence-not-one-dimensional.md)); domain-by-domain superiority asks only for
the conjunction of such comparisons. To land, then, the objection must do more than deny a single
scalar: it must further show that the domain-by-domain reading is *also* ill-defined or
unsatisfiable — for instance that "all domains of interest" cannot be enumerated without covert
appeal to an overall ranking, or that superiority within a domain is itself not well-ordered. That
further step is the argument's own weakest link; without it, the physical-possibility claim can
sidestep the objection by disclaiming any scalar and reading its own quantifier domain-wise, as its
body already does.

### In plain terms

People picture intelligence as a single dial you can turn up, so that "superintelligence" just means
turning it past the human maximum. This objection says that picture is a myth: minds aren't ranked on
one scale, they're good and bad at *different* things, the way a hawk, an octopus, and a chess
computer are each brilliant in their own way with no fact about who is "smartest overall." If that's
right, then "smarter than every human at everything, period" isn't false — it's a sentence with no
clear meaning, so there's nothing definite for it to be possible or impossible ([superintelligence-physically-possible](../claims/superintelligence-physically-possible.md)).
The honest catch, which the objection has to answer: the standard definition of superintelligence
doesn't actually say "one dial." It says "beats the best humans in nearly *every* area" — area by
area. You can make sense of that list even if the areas don't add up to a single number. So the
objection only really bites if it can go on to show that even the area-by-area version breaks down,
and that is the hard part.
