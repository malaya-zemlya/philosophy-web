This argument gathers a family of cases in which the felt quality of experience behaves exactly as
it should if it were constituted by relations, and not as it should if it were an intrinsic property
known directly. It concludes, as the best explanation, that qualia are wholly relational
([qualia-are-relational](../claims/qualia-are-relational.md)), and so attacks the thesis, basic to many theories of
consciousness, that experience is intrinsic ([consciousness-is-intrinsic-property](../claims/consciousness-is-intrinsic-property.md)).

### The evidence

The inference form is abductive. Each case is one in which an intrinsic-quality view predicts that
the subject should have firm access to the quality, and the subject does not.

1. **Misidentified modality.** A subject can sincerely experience an activity as composing music
   while it is in fact drawing a picture, and under dissociative drugs cannot tell whether an
   experience is a sound or an image ([activity-kind-misexperienced](../claims/activity-kind-misexperienced.md)). Which sense an
   experience belongs to is its coarsest feature. If even that is not given directly, it is plausibly
   fixed by relations: where the experience comes from, how it is structured, and what one can do
   with it. Those are the relations a dissociative drug disrupts.
2. **Unity that comes apart.** In fatigue, anxiety or severe ADHD, experience can present as loosely
   interacting sub-processes, seeming unified only in retrospect
   ([degraded-states-orchestratorless](../claims/degraded-states-orchestratorless.md)). The *togetherness* of experience, which an intrinsic
   view treats as a basic given, varies with the integration that relates the parts.
3. **Indistinguishable changes.** In Dennett's case of two coffee tasters who no longer like their
   coffee, one says the taste is the same but his liking changed, the other that the taste itself
   changed. Nothing available to either, including his own introspection, settles which is right
   ([dennett-1988](../sources/dennett-1988.md)). Synaesthesia poses the same question: when a sound triggers a colour,
   has a quality changed, or only the relations between familiar qualities? Every test the subject
   could apply (comparing with memory, judging similarities, seeing what the experience lets one do)
   is itself relational.
4. **Fallible introspection in general.** Introspection is a constructed and fallible
   representation of experience, not a direct presentation of it
   ([introspection-is-fallible-representation](../claims/introspection-is-fallible-representation.md); [schwitzgebel-2008](../sources/schwitzgebel-2008.md)).
5. **What first contact teaches.** When a system meets a stimulus for the first time, what it gains
   is the stimulus's place among its expectations: what brings it on and what follows from it. That
   is so in a language model, by the mechanics of training, and by analogy in Mary seeing red
   ([mary-gains-relational-placement](../claims/mary-gains-relational-placement.md)).

**Conclusion.** The best explanation of these cases is that the felt quality of an experience is
constituted by its relations. When the relations shift, fragment or are cut, the quality shifts,
fragments or becomes indeterminate, and introspection, itself a relational process, tracks only the
relations.

### What is at stake

The rival thesis, that experience is intrinsic, is a premise of the [intrinsic-nature](intrinsic-nature.md),
and through it of [russellian-monism](../positions/russellian-monism.md), [panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md) and
[panqualityism](../positions/panqualityism.md). The related thesis that phenomenal states are not exhausted by their
functional role underlies [zombie-conceivability](zombie-conceivability.md), absent-qualia and inverted-spectrum
arguments, and [dualism](../positions/dualism.md). If this argument succeeds, each of these loses a premise.

### The weakest premise

The weakest step is the move from what introspection can detect to what experience is. The
defender of intrinsic qualia can grant every case and reply that the intrinsic quality is real but
*inaccessible* in these conditions. The drug, the fatigue and the coffee tasters show only that
introspection is fallible, not that there is nothing for it to be wrong about. This reply draws on the
claims that qualia can be inaccessible ([qualia-can-be-inaccessible](../claims/qualia-can-be-inaccessible.md)) and that a subject can be
wrong about their own qualia ([subject-can-be-wrong-about-own-qualia](../claims/subject-can-be-wrong-about-own-qualia.md)).

The reply is that such a quality would explain nothing. It would make no difference to what the
subject can notice, report, compare or do, and no difference that anyone else could observe. Positing
it is the very point in dispute, and an unobservable residue of this kind is best treated as idle
([real-feel-residue-is-idle](real-feel-residue-is-idle.md)). A second objection is that relations need relata with a
nature of their own ([relations-grounded-in-intrinsic](../claims/relations-grounded-in-intrinsic.md)). The relational view can accept
that the relata are neural states with physical natures. What it denies is that their *qualitative*
character is among those natures.

### In plain terms

Many theories of consciousness assume that how red looks to you is a quality the experience has all
by itself, which you know directly just by having it. This argument collects cases where that seems to
fail. On a drug that scrambles the senses, someone can't tell whether they're hearing or seeing, and
thinks they're writing music when they're drawing a picture. When very tired, experience stops feeling
like one whole. Two coffee drinkers can't tell whether the taste changed or only their liking for it.
In each case, the "feel" changes or blurs exactly when the experience's connections to everything else
change. The simplest explanation is that the feel just *is* those connections. Defenders reply that
the real quality might still be there, just hidden. Critics answer that a quality nobody can ever
detect, not even the person having it, does no work.
