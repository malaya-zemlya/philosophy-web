The **parity argument for extended consciousness** holds that whoever accepts the reasoning behind the
[extended mind](../concepts/extended-mind.md) has no principled ground for stopping at cognition. It was set
out by Karina Vold ([vold-2015](../sources/vold-2015.md)) against the view, held by Andy Clark and David Chalmers
themselves, that cognition can extend while consciousness stays in the head
([extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness](../claims/extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness.md)).

**Inference form:** analogy (parity of cases).

1. **Parity.** On the reasoning that supports the extended mind, an external process that plays the
   role an internal process plays in cognition counts as part of the cognitive process, whatever its
   location ([clark-chalmers-1998](../sources/clark-chalmers-1998.md)).
2. **The grounds for denial are differences of degree.** The reasons given for denying that
   consciousness extends, that external loops are too slow, too narrow in bandwidth or too indirect
   ([clark-2009](../sources/clark-2009.md), [chalmers-2019](../sources/chalmers-2019.md)), describe contingent features of present-day
   external resources, not something that an external process could not in principle overcome.
3. ∴ An external process coupled to a subject quickly and richly enough to play the role an internal
   process plays in conscious experience would, by the same parity, be part of the physical basis of
   that experience. Extended cognition and extended consciousness stand or fall together.

### Weakest premise

The weak point is premise 2, and the analogy scheme's disanalogy question (CQ2) is where it is
attacked. The defender of the non-entailment claim can reply that speed, bandwidth and direct access
are not incidental features of conscious processing but part of the role it plays. On that reply no
external process could play the role while keeping the feature that makes it external, namely being
reached through perception and action. A second line of reply targets premise 1 for consciousness in
particular: if phenomenal character is not exhausted by functional role, as many anti-physicalists
hold, then parity of role does not settle parity of experience, though this reply is not open to the
functionalists who are the argument's main audience.

### In plain terms

If you accept that a notebook can be part of your memory because it does the job memory does, this
argument asks why something outside your head couldn't equally be part of your *experience* if it did
the job the brain does there. The usual answer, that the outside world is connected to you too slowly
and loosely, describes how things happen to be, not how they must be. Critics reply that speed and
directness aren't a side detail of experience but part of what it is.
