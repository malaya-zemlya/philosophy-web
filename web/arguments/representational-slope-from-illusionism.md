The **representational slope from illusionism** argues that illusionism, combined with the
representationalist account of consciousness it most naturally relies on, cannot keep consciousness
inside brains. The argument is a slippery slope of the continuity kind
([continuity-argument-schema](../concepts/continuity-argument-schema.md)), but it is run on the illusionist's own terms. It does not
assume that phenomenal properties exist. It asks how far the real thing the illusionist puts in
their place extends.

**Inference form:** deductive, as a dilemma ([dilemma pattern](../../patterns/dilemma.md)).

1. Illusionism is a negative thesis. It must be paired with a positive account of what
   consciousness is, and the account that fits it best says that what is real in consciousness is
   representation; only its seeming special is illusory ([illusionism-is-negative-thesis](../claims/illusionism-is-negative-thesis.md),
   [representationalism](../positions/representationalism.md)).
2. Either the notion of representation is left unrestricted, or it is restricted.
3. *Horn A.* Unrestricted, representation is satisfied by causal dependence alone, and so by every
   physical system ([causal-dependence-representation-trivial](../claims/causal-dependence-representation-trivial.md)).
4. *Horn B.* Restricted by the standard that keeps physics from triviality, with failure cases and
   a mapping fixed in advance ([nontrivial-applicability-requires-failure-cases](../claims/nontrivial-applicability-requires-failure-cases.md)), it still
   includes photographic plates. Restricted further by a requirement that the state be *used*, it
   reaches at least feedback controllers or single cells, on every available reading of "use"
   ([use-criteria-regress](../claims/use-criteria-regress.md)).
5. ∴ On either horn, consciousness in the illusionist's sense is not confined to brains
   ([illusionist-representationalism-not-brain-bound](../claims/illusionist-representationalism-not-brain-bound.md)).

On Horn A the conclusion is a deflationary panpsychism. On Horn B it is a form of biopsychism. A
precedent for accepting such a spread is David Chalmers's suggestion, on an information-based view,
that a thermostat may have a very simple experience ([Chalmers, 1996](../sources/chalmers-1996.md)), and
Daniel Dennett's acceptance that the intentional stance applies, feebly, to a thermostat
([intentional-stance](../positions/intentional-stance.md), [Dennett, 1987](../sources/dennett-1987.md)).

### Why the slope does not trivialize physics

An objection is that the same reasoning would show that any mathematical model applies to anything,
which would deny the success of ordinary physics. The argument avoids this by conceding that a
non-trivial concept must be able to fail, under a mapping fixed in advance, as Newton's concepts can.
Representation in that disciplined sense is what Horn B considers. The slope does not depend on the
loose reading being the only one.

### Weakest premise

The weak point is premise 4, the claim that every non-trivial reading of "use" reaches far beyond
brains, which the dilemma pattern's CQ-horn-link targets. An illusionist can propose a reading not yet
considered, or can accept the etiological reading together with its consequence that a historyless
duplicate is not conscious. Premise 1 is also open. An illusionist may choose a positive theory that
is not representationalist, such as a global-workspace account, but that choice moves the problem to
the grain of the workspace rather than removing it ([illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma](illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma.md)).
The conclusion concerns only the *distribution* of consciousness. It does not give the realist
panpsychist the intrinsic phenomenal properties they assert ([panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md)).

### In plain terms

Illusionists say the "inner glow" of experience is a trick the brain plays on itself, and that what
is really there is the brain representing things. But many things represent, at least in a modest
sense. Film records light, and bacteria steer by chemical gradients. If representation is all that
consciousness really is, these things have the real part of consciousness and are simply not fooled
about it. Tightening the meaning of "representation" narrows the circle, but every tightening on offer
still leaves it far wider than brains.
