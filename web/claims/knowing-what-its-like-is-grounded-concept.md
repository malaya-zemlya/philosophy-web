To know what it is like to see red is to have a concept of red that is grounded in one's own colour
processing. Before her release Mary has a complete *propositional* concept of red, from books and
black-and-white films. On release her sensory cortex is configured by colour, and her higher,
associative regions gain a link to that configuration, which Michael Beaton calls the concept
*red_as_experienced* ([beaton-2005](../sources/beaton-2005.md), pp. 13–14). Both parts are needed. Low-level colour
discrimination without the link to reasoning and report would be like blindsight, and the link with
nothing below it would ground nothing.

The analysis develops the ability replies of David Lewis and Paul Churchland
([ability-hypothesis](../arguments/ability-hypothesis.md)). Like them it makes Mary's gain physical. It differs from
Churchland in holding that Mary gains a new way of thinking about the world rather than direct
knowledge of her own brain state.

### In plain terms

Knowing what red looks like means that your thinking about red is hooked up to the parts of your
brain that actually detect red. Book knowledge gives you a concept of red, but not one connected to
your own colour vision. Seeing red makes that connection.
