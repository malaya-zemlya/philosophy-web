The physics of the present day does not yet give a complete and correct inventory of the world's
basic entities, properties and laws, and some of what it now says will turn out to be false.
Hempel ([1980](../sources/hempel-1980.md)) makes the point as a premise of [hempels-dilemma](../arguments/hempels-dilemma.md): if
"physical" means "posited by current physics", then the thesis that everything is physical is very
probably false, whatever the truth about minds.

**Scope.** The claim concerns physics as a body of theory at a particular date. It does not say that
current physics is badly wrong, only that it is not final. The standard evidence is internal to
physics itself: quantum theory and general relativity have not been unified and do not fit together
at extreme scales, and the nature of dark matter and dark energy remains open.

**Who denies it.** Few deny the premise outright. Defenders of the first horn of the dilemma, such as
Melnyk ([1997](../sources/melnyk-1997.md)), grant it and deny only that it makes a current-physics physicalism
unreasonable to believe.

Distinct from [future-physics-unspecifiable](future-physics-unspecifiable.md), which concerns what *later* physics will
contain, not whether present physics is final.
