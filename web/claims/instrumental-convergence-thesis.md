For almost any final goal, a sufficiently capable agent has instrumental reason to preserve itself,
protect its goal content from alteration, improve its own capabilities, and acquire resources —
because each of these serves nearly every end an agent could have. This is the *thesis*, an asserted
and contestable proposition, as distinct from the phenomenon it is about, which
[instrumental-convergence](../concepts/instrumental-convergence.md) defines ([omohundro-2008](../sources/omohundro-2008.md); systematized in
[bostrom-2012](../sources/bostrom-2012.md)).

### Scope

The claim is about *reasons generated by means-end structure*, not a prediction that every system
acts on them: a weak or tightly constrained agent may have the instrumental reason and still be
unable to act on it. And the convergence is *defeasible* — a final goal that itself values deference,
corrigibility, or shutdown-compliance cancels the corresponding drive, since for such an agent
allowing correction is part of what it is ultimately trying to do. The thesis says the subgoals
converge across almost all ends, not that they are inescapable whatever the end.

### Who would deny it

Relatively few dispute the thesis on its own terms, since it follows fairly directly from the
structure of goal-pursuit. Skeptics more often reject a surrounding premise: that real systems will
be capable, agentic optimizers of fixed final goals at all — denying the antecedent rather than the
conditional.
