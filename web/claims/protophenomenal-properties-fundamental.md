The most basic physical entities have intrinsic *protophenomenal* properties. These are not forms of
consciousness, and nothing at the fundamental level is a subject of experience, but suitable combinations
of them constitute experience. The constitution is meant to be *transparent*: someone who knew all the
facts about the protophenomenal properties of a brain could in principle work out, a priori, what it is
like to be that brain's owner. This is the core thesis of *panprotopsychism*, a name and a framework due
to David Chalmers [chalmers-2003](../sources/chalmers-2003.md), [chalmers-2017](../sources/chalmers-2017.md).

The transparency condition is what gives the claim content. Every view on which consciousness is not
fundamental holds that it is built from *some* other properties, so "precursors of experience" alone
would make ordinary physicalism count as panprotopsychism. What the claim adds is that the fundamental
properties are of a special kind, not captured by physics' structural description, from which experience
follows intelligibly rather than as a brute fact [sep-panpsychism](../sources/sep-panpsychism.md).

The claim is distinct from [consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous](consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous.md), which places *experience* itself,
with micro-subjects to have it, at the fundamental level. The difference matters for the
[combination-problem](../arguments/combination-problem.md): since the protophenomenal claim posits no micro-subjects, the objection
that [micro-subjects-do-not-sum](micro-subjects-do-not-sum.md) does not bear on it. Its own standing difficulty is the step from
non-experiential properties to a subject's awareness, which critics think reopens the explanatory gap one
level down. [qualities-exist-unexperienced](qualities-exist-unexperienced.md) is one specific version of the claim, on which the
protophenomenal properties are qualities of the kind found in experience, existing unexperienced.

Physicalists of the usual kind deny the claim, since they hold that experience is constituted by the
properties physics describes. Panpsychists in the strict sense deny it as an account of the fundamental
level, since they hold that the fundamental properties are already experiential.

### In plain terms

This is the idea that the tiniest bits of matter have special hidden features that are not feelings
themselves, but that add up to feelings when they are combined the right way, much as atoms that are not
alive add up to living things. It is a more cautious cousin of the view that tiny bits of matter
actually have tiny experiences (that view is [consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous](consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous.md)). Because it puts
no little experiencers at the bottom, it avoids the puzzle of how many little minds could merge into one
big mind. The open question is whether such feeling-free ingredients really could add up to a feeling.
