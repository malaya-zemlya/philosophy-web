For any physical effect that is caused at all, there is a wholly physical cause that suffices to bring
it about. One never needs to look outside the physical domain to find a sufficient cause of a physical
happening. This is the [completeness-of-physics](../concepts/completeness-of-physics.md) stated as a single premise.

"Sufficient" is the operative word. The claim does not say that the physical cause is the *only*
cause, only that it is *enough*, and so it leaves open, until [no-systematic-overdetermination](no-systematic-overdetermination.md)
is added, that a physical effect might also have a non-physical cause. To accommodate indeterminism it
is read as a claim about chances: in so far as the probability of a physical effect is fixed at all, it
is fixed by physical antecedents.

Its defenders treat it as an empirical result rather than an a priori axiom. David Papineau
([papineau-2002](../sources/papineau-2002.md)) argues that it was established by the historical closing of the inventory of
fundamental forces and that it is underwritten by the conservation laws. Interactionist
[dualism](../positions/dualism.md) is the principal view that rejects it.
