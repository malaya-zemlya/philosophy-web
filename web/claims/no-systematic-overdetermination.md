When a mental event causes a physical effect, that effect is not also brought about by a distinct and
independently sufficient non-physical cause running alongside the physical one. Bodily movements are
not routinely caused twice over, the way a death might be overdetermined by two simultaneous fatal
wounds. Such a coincidence might happen once by fluke, but it does not happen systematically, for every
voluntary action of every agent.

The claim rules out only *systematic* overdetermination, not the odd genuine case. Its work is to close
the gap that the [completeness-of-physics](../concepts/completeness-of-physics.md) leaves open. Completeness guarantees a sufficient
physical cause, and this premise denies a second, non-physical one standing beside it, so that together
they yield the [causal-closure](../concepts/causal-closure.md) of the physical.

It is the premise an interactionist dualist is most tempted to challenge, either by embracing
overdetermination or by denying that the physical cause was sufficient after all. Jaegwon Kim
([kim-2005](../sources/kim-2005.md)) builds it into the exclusion principle at the heart of his
[causal-exclusion](../arguments/causal-exclusion.md), and it is also a premise of the
[causal-argument-for-physicalism](../arguments/causal-argument-for-physicalism.md).
