Each increment of intelligence buys less than the last, so recursive self-improvement yields
sigmoidal growth that levels off rather than the exponential runaway of an
[intelligence-explosion](../concepts/intelligence-explosion.md). Two pressures do the flattening: the problems a smarter
designer must solve grow harder as it climbs, and progress is bottlenecked by the environment,
the available data, and the sheer time real-world experiments take — not by the designer's raw
intellect, which cannot be spent faster than the world will answer ([chollet-2017](../sources/chollet-2017.md)).

The claim denies the *runaway dynamics*, not the arrival of very capable AI. It is fully
compatible with systems that are far more capable than today's, or even than humans, provided
they come gradually rather than through a self-amplifying spike; what it rejects is the
compounding feedback loop, not advanced machine intelligence as such. It is the direct rival of
[self-improvement-leads-to-explosion](self-improvement-leads-to-explosion.md), meeting that thesis at its proportionality premise
by holding the returns to be sharply diminishing.
