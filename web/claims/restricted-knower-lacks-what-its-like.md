The Mary intuition, that Mary would not know what seeing red is like before her release, is true of
the agent the knowledge argument actually describes: a knower like us in the way we come to know
what colours look like, and enhanced only in knowing and reasoning about the physical facts. Such an
agent can work out every detail of what it would say and do after seeing red, and still not be in
the state of knowing what it is like ([beaton-2005](../sources/beaton-2005.md), pp. 23–24).

The claim is compatible with physicalism, and Michael Beaton, who defends it, is a physicalist. It
denies only that physicalism requires every complete physical knower to be able to come to know
what it is like without the experience. Agents with further abilities, such as writing to their own
colour registers, could do so. That shows their architecture is different from ours, not that
physicalism is false.

The claim is narrower than the knowledge argument's own premise that Mary learns something on
release ([mary-learns-on-release](mary-learns-on-release.md)). It concerns an agent restricted to the abilities her
knowledge entails, and it carries no suggestion that what she lacks is a non-physical fact.

### In plain terms

Even a perfect scientist, who knows everything physical about colour vision and can reason
flawlessly about it, would not know what red looks like until seeing it, unless they also had some
extra power to rewire their own brain. This is a fact about how minds like ours are built, not
evidence for anything non-physical.
