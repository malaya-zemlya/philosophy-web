According to Igor Mikhailov, the missing link in consciousness science is an "engineering level": a
functional scheme built from a finite set of standard elements that can be read both in terms of the
theory's laws and in terms of empirical descriptions ([Mikhailov, 2024](../sources/mikhailov-2024.md)). The
best candidate for that level, he argues, is computational models, taken as economical descriptions
of complex, multistable, self-regulating systems. Since learning and evolution must be accounted for,
the models should be probabilistic rather than deterministic. The claim answers the diagnosis in
[ncc-programme-lacks-causal-hypotheses](ncc-programme-lacks-causal-hypotheses.md).

Vadim Vasilyev accepts the proposal of a computational level with real modelling power, while
disputing Mikhailov's use of falsifiability as a criterion for empirical theories
([Vasilyev, 2024](../sources/vasilyev-2024.md); [falsifiability-needs-weak-direct-verifiability](falsifiability-needs-weak-direct-verifiability.md)).
The claim is distinct from [theory-of-consciousness-must-meet-minimal-requirements](theory-of-consciousness-must-meet-minimal-requirements.md), which
lists what a theory must explain rather than what form it must take.
