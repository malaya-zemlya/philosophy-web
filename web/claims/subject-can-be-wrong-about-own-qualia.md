A person can be mistaken about what their own experience is like, even while having it and attending
to it carefully. Sincere judgements of the form "my experience now is like *this*" are not guaranteed
true. Eric Schwitzgebel argues that naive introspection of current conscious experience is unreliable,
and that people err even about its gross features ([schwitzgebel-2008](../sources/schwitzgebel-2008.md)). His examples include
how much of the visual field is clear at once, whether thought comes with imagery, and what emotions
feel like.

### Scope

The claim is about the *character* of experience, not its existence. It is compatible with holding that
one cannot be wrong that one is experiencing something at all. It is also compatible with realism about
qualia: Schwitzgebel takes experience to be real and introspection to be a poor instrument for studying
it.

It is distinct from [introspection-is-fallible-representation](introspection-is-fallible-representation.md), Daniel Dennett's thesis that
introspection is a constructed representation of experience. That is a theory of what introspection
*is*, and is usually paired with the view that the feel it represents is partly illusory. This claim is
the bare epistemic thesis that error is possible, neutral on the mechanism and on whether qualia are
real. It is distinct from [qualia-can-be-inaccessible](qualia-can-be-inaccessible.md), since it concerns a false verdict rather
than none. Experimental support comes from the
confabulation studies ([introspective-confabulation](../arguments/introspective-confabulation.md)). A case of it is
[activity-kind-misexperienced](activity-kind-misexperienced.md), in which a subject misjudged even the
sense modality of what they were doing.

### What it opposes

The claim directly denies the datum answer to the question of acquaintance,
[introspective-acquaintance-is-datum](introspective-acquaintance-is-datum.md), on which introspection meets the experience itself and so
cannot be wrong wholesale, and Searle's argument that for experience the appearance is the reality
([no-appearance-reality-gap](../arguments/no-appearance-reality-gap.md)). It also limits [privileged-first-person-access](privileged-first-person-access.md): access
may be direct without being infallible. It is compatible with [phenomenal-determinacy](phenomenal-determinacy.md), since there
must be a determinate fact for one to be wrong about. It undercuts the many arguments that take reliable self-knowledge of qualia
as given; these are set out in [fallible-access-undercuts-qualia-arguments](../arguments/fallible-access-undercuts-qualia-arguments.md). Like the claim
that qualia can be inaccessible, it is the defender of intrinsic qualia's natural reply to
[qualia-relational-from-cases](../arguments/qualia-relational-from-cases.md), and it meets the same rejoinder: if the true character of an
experience is something no one, including its owner, can reliably detect, it is in danger of being an
idle residue ([real-feel-residue-is-idle](../arguments/real-feel-residue-is-idle.md)).

### In plain terms

We usually think that, whatever else we're wrong about, we can't be wrong about how things seem to us
right now. This claim says we can. People turn out to be confidently mistaken about basic features of
their own experience, such as how much of what they see is actually sharp.
