There is no single axis on which minds can be totally ordered from less to more intelligent.
Cognition is instead a possibility-space of distinct, partly incommensurable capacities —
perception, memory, planning, language, social insight, motor control, and many more — so that
"smarter than a human across the board" has no well-defined referent ([kelly-2017](../sources/kelly-2017.md)).
Minds occupy different regions of the space; ranking them by one number is like ranking animals
by a single "size."

The claim is about the *structure of the concept and its measure*, not about what machines can or
cannot do. It is fully compatible with a system that exceeds humans on any given capacity, or on
many at once; what it denies is that these capacities share a common scale on which a total
across-the-board ranking could be read off — the [intelligence](../concepts/intelligence.md) entry's third,
multidimensional-bundle sense, as against its scalar, optimization-power sense. It is the engine
of [multidimensionality-objection](../arguments/multidimensionality-objection.md), which turns this structural point against the very
statability of superhuman intelligence.

It is denied by those who treat general optimization power as a usable scalar — sense 1 of
[intelligence](../concepts/intelligence.md) — on which minds can, at least in principle, be ordered.
