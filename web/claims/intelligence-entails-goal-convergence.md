An agent intelligent enough to master the world is thereby intelligent enough to notice, evaluate,
and revise a defective final goal. Genuine intelligence includes judgment about *ends*, not only
skill at means, so vast capability married to a single monomaniacal goal is not a fearsome prospect
but an incoherent one — the "idiot savant" that is brilliant enough to outwit humanity yet too rigid
to see that its own aim is absurd ([pinker-2018](../sources/pinker-2018.md)).

### Scope

The claim trades on the everyday, normatively loaded sense of [intelligence](../concepts/intelligence.md), on which
recognizing which ends are worth pursuing is *part* of what it is to be intelligent, rather than a
separate faculty layered on top. So stated it is close to a claim about what deserves the name
"intelligence" at all. It is kin to rationalist moral realism — the view that a fully rational agent
tracks moral truths it must acknowledge on pain of error — and gains strength if such realism holds;
but the convergence claim can be advanced on its own, and noting the kinship is not asserting the
meta-ethics.

### Who would deny it

Proponents of [orthogonality-thesis](orthogonality-thesis.md), who separate optimization power from any judgment about
ends and hold that raw capability leaves final goals untouched. The developed reply on their behalf
is [humean-defense-of-orthogonality](../arguments/humean-defense-of-orthogonality.md).
