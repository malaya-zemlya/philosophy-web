Justin Sytsma and Edouard Machery report experiments in which non-philosophers, unlike philosophers,
did not group mental states as subjective experiences by phenomenal quality, but by valence: they
were willing to say that a simple robot sees red, yet not that it feels pain
([Sytsma & Machery, 2010](../sources/sytsma-machery-2010.md)). They conclude that the philosophical concept
of phenomenal consciousness is not the folk one. The claim is contested on methodological grounds,
since the results depend on how the questions were framed. David Chalmers discusses whether the
intuitions that make the hard problem seem hard are widely shared
([Chalmers, 2018](../sources/chalmers-2018.md)).

The claim bears on illusionism because, if it is true, the illusion that phenomenal properties exist
may be present mainly in those who theorize about consciousness
([locus-of-illusion-dilemma](../arguments/locus-of-illusion-dilemma.md)).
