At least some mental events make a difference to the physical world. Decisions issue in bodily
movements, pains prompt winces, and perceptions guide reaching hands. The mind is not a mere spectator
but is among the causes of what bodies do.

The claim asserts only that mental causation reaches the physical world. It does not say how, whether
by identity, by realisation or through some other relation, nor how many mental events are
efficacious. It is the premise that thoroughgoing [epiphenomenalism](../positions/epiphenomenalism.md) denies, holding the
mental to be a causally inert by-product ([mental-states-causally-inert](mental-states-causally-inert.md)).

Physicalists and dualists alike mostly accept it as a near-datum of common sense, and that is what
gives the [causal-argument-for-physicalism](../arguments/causal-argument-for-physicalism.md) its grip. Combined with the
[completeness-of-physics](../concepts/completeness-of-physics.md), a mind that genuinely moves the body is pressed toward being
physical.
