If a state represents whatever it is a lossy function of, then every physical state represents,
because every physical state is a lossy function of its causal past. The retina represents light,
but so do the silver-halide crystals of a photographic plate, the warming of a stone in the sun,
and the deformation of a mountain by an earthquake. The notion forbids nothing, since the condition
it imposes is guaranteed by causation itself. A theory of consciousness that identifies consciousness
with representation in this sense makes consciousness universal.

The claim concerns one notion of representation, not representation in general. It is distinct
from [intentionality-is-stance-relative](intentionality-is-stance-relative.md), which says that content is fixed by an
interpreter's stance. Here content is fixed objectively, but too cheaply. Restricted notions that
avoid the triviality are taken up in [nontrivial-applicability-requires-failure-cases](nontrivial-applicability-requires-failure-cases.md) and
[use-criteria-regress](use-criteria-regress.md).

### In plain terms

If "representing something" just means "being affected by it and keeping some trace", then
everything represents, because everything is affected by its surroundings and keeps traces of them.
A footprint represents a foot. A theory that says consciousness is just representation, in that
loose sense, would make footprints conscious.
