If a human person survives death at all, it is because the person is raised again as a living bodily
being, not because some immaterial part of the person carries on while the body decays. The claim
follows from denying that persons have a separable soul while affirming the Christian hope of life after
death, and it is the shared commitment of Christian physicalists such as Peter van Inwagen
[van-inwagen-1978](../sources/van-inwagen-1978.md), Lynne Rudder Baker [baker-2000](../sources/baker-2000.md) and Nancey Murphy
[murphy-2006](../sources/murphy-2006.md). Its chief difficulty is identity: if the body is destroyed and later
reconstituted, what makes the raised person the same person rather than a perfect copy?

The claim is denied by substance dualists, who hold that the soul survives the body's death
([soul-is-immaterial-substance](soul-is-immaterial-substance.md)), and in a qualified way by Thomists, for whom the soul subsists
between death and resurrection though the complete person does not.
