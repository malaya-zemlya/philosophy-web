Every mental event, whether a thought, a sensation, a decision or an experience, is identical to or
wholly constituted by some physical event. There is no mental occurrence over and above the physical
goings-on of the brain and body.

The claim concerns *token* mental events: each particular mental happening is some particular physical
happening. It does not by itself assert *type* identity, the thesis that pain as a kind is one physical
kind, and a functionalist or non-reductive physicalist can accept the token claim while denying the type
claim. Nor does it settle why a given physical event feels as it does. Granting the identity is
compatible with conceding a [hard-problem](../concepts/hard-problem.md) of explanation.

[dualism](../positions/dualism.md) denies the claim outright, holding that the mental is non-physical.
[epiphenomenalism](../positions/epiphenomenalism.md) also rejects it, since it grants that the mental is non-physical and adds
only that it is causally inert. The claim is the conclusion of the
[causal-argument-for-physicalism](../arguments/causal-argument-for-physicalism.md) and the core commitment of [physicalism](../positions/physicalism.md).

It is distinct from [mental-states-are-brain-state-types](mental-states-are-brain-state-types.md), which asserts the stronger
type-identity thesis that mental kinds are physical kinds. The present claim is the weaker token-level
identity that even non-reductive physicalists accept.
