Imagine a man created all at once and fully formed, with his sight veiled, suspended in empty air so
that nothing touches him, and his limbs held apart so that they do not touch each other. He would have no
sensation of anything, including his own body. Avicenna claims that such a man would nonetheless affirm
that he exists, while affirming nothing about his limbs, organs or any external thing
[avicenna-de-anima](../sources/avicenna-de-anima.md), [marmura-1986](../sources/marmura-1986.md). Self-awareness, on this view, does not depend
on any awareness of the body.

The claim is the first premise of [floating-man](../arguments/floating-man.md). Critics accept it more readily than the
conclusion drawn from it, since the step from "aware of myself without being aware of my body" to "I am
not my body" is the contested one.
