To reason well from what one knows is one ability. To re-engineer, at will, the mechanisms that carry
one's reasoning is another, and nothing in the first entails the second. A system whose high-level
knowledge is supported by lower-level processing can know everything about that processing without
being able, by thinking alone, to make it run in the right causal relation to its reasoning
([beaton-2005](../sources/beaton-2005.md), pp. 20, 22–23). The knowledge *admits* such an ability but does not *entail*
it. Humans plainly lack it: no amount of colour science lets a person set up their own colour cortex
by reflection.

The claim is the general form of a point also made about learning systems: an agent that can write
to its own representations can install in advance what an encounter would provide
([write-access-installs-binding](write-access-installs-binding.md)), but that write access is an extra ability, not part of
knowing.

### In plain terms

Understanding exactly how your own brain works is different from being able to rewire it by
thinking. A person could know everything about how their brain handles colour and still be unable
to make it behave as if it had seen red.
