Igor Mikhailov argues that directly matching phenomenal, introspective descriptions against
neurophysiological data has not produced a theory of consciousness, because the search for
[neural correlates](../concepts/neural-correlates-of-consciousness.md), introduced by Francis Crick and
Christof Koch ([Crick & Koch, 1990](../sources/crick-koch-1990.md)), lacks working hypotheses about the
causal link between correlates and conscious states ([Mikhailov, 2024](../sources/mikhailov-2024.md)). He
adds a second obstacle, the multiplicity of explananda. It is unclear whether the target is conscious
states or their contents, and whether access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness are one
capacity or two that merely share a name. He compares the situation to Yuri Lazebnik's biologist
trying to fix a radio by cataloguing its parts ([Lazebnik, 2002](../sources/lazebnik-2002.md)): a blind
search for correlations between vague, unverifiable concepts and unstructured data.

The claim is about the state of a research programme, not about whether consciousness is in principle
empirically tractable. Its remedy is stated in [consciousness-science-needs-engineering-level](consciousness-science-needs-engineering-level.md).
