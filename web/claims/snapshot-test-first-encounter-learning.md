Whether a learner learns anything from a first encounter can be settled by comparing three snapshots
of it: one just before the encounter, one just after, and one long after, once any slow consolidation
has finished. Differences between the first two that are gone by the third belong to *having* the
experience, as an activation passes through a network or a perception through a brain. Differences
that remain at the third are lasting changes in the learner's connections, and a lasting change caused
by the encounter is learning, by definition.

Any experience leaves some lasting trace, if only a memory that it happened. So the comparison is made
against a baseline: the lasting change the same encounter produces in a learner already familiar with
the item, such as a person who has seen red all their life seeing a red tomato once more. The *excess*
over that baseline is what the first encounter specifically teaches. For a language model the excess is
directly measurable, as the difference between the large training update on a token's first occurrence
and the small one on its hundredth ([llmary](../concepts/llmary.md)).

The test makes several earlier verdicts precise. A learner whose relevant circuitry is wholly fixed in
advance shows no excess, and so learns nothing specific from the encounter
([mary-gain-depends-on-prewiring](mary-gain-depends-on-prewiring.md)). A learner that has already installed the change by writing
its own parameters also shows no excess at the encounter itself, because the change came earlier
([write-access-installs-binding](write-access-installs-binding.md)). And what [first-encounter-adds-binding](first-encounter-adds-binding.md) says is gained
is the excess, located in the learner's representation of the item.

The test concerns learning in the physical sense, a lasting change in the learner. Whether a learner
can also come to *know* something that no such change records is the further question taken up in
[retention-dilemma](../arguments/retention-dilemma.md).

### In plain terms

To tell whether meeting something for the first time taught you anything, compare your brain just
before, just after, and long after. Whatever is still different long after, beyond what meeting the
thing for the hundredth time would leave, is what you learned. If nothing is different, you learned
nothing from it.
