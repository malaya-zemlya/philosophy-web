Research on the mind-body problem, including the search for neural correlates of consciousness,
usually concerns relations across levels, between conscious states and brain states. Matvey Sysoev
argues that it must also take in the *vertical* relations of part to whole studied under the headings
of unity and binding, because conscious states come both as one overall state and as many specific
states, and an account of either must say how they relate
([Sysoev, 2025](../sources/sysoev-2025.md); [vertical-and-horizontal-problems-of-consciousness](../concepts/vertical-and-horizontal-problems-of-consciousness.md)).
Neglecting this connection is what produces the mereological fallacy and the conflation of levels
([mereological-fallacy](../concepts/mereological-fallacy.md), [level-of-abstraction-conflation](../concepts/level-of-abstraction-conflation.md)). The practical upshot
is that how the problems intersect should shape which hypotheses are formulated within the
neural-correlates programme.

The claim is methodological. It does not say how the problems are to be solved, and it is compatible
with any metaphysics of mind. It would be denied by approaches that treat the correlates of specific
contents as separable from the correlates of being conscious at all.
