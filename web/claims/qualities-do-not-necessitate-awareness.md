However qualities are instantiated or arranged, the facts about them do not by themselves necessitate
that anyone is *aware* of them. In David Chalmers's words, "no instantiations of qualities ever
necessitate awareness of qualities" [chalmers-2017](../sources/chalmers-2017.md). One can conceive of a world containing all
the same qualities, organized in the same way, in which nothing is experienced, a world of
*quality zombies*. If that is possible, then the step from unexperienced qualities to experienced ones
leaves an explanatory gap of the same kind as the one between physical structure and consciousness.

The claim is the principal objection to [panqualityism](../positions/panqualityism.md), whose
[unexperienced qualities](qualities-exist-unexperienced.md) must somehow become experienced. It is
denied by panqualityists such as Sam Coleman, who argue that awareness can be analysed, for example as a
higher-order state that embeds the quality itself, in a way that closes the gap
[coleman-2015](../sources/coleman-2015.md).
