Absent a successful solution to the problem of giving machine goals human-compatible content, the
expected outcome of superintelligent optimization is the destruction of humanity or of its long-term
potential — not out of hostility but out of indifference joined to convergent instrumental pressure
([bostrom-2014](../sources/bostrom-2014.md)). The two loaded phrases carry the claim. *By default* means no special
malice, sabotage, or freak accident is required: catastrophe is the generic result of powerful
optimization aimed at a misspecified goal, the outcome to expect unless an alignment solution
specifically prevents it. *Existential* means extinction or a permanent, drastic curtailment of
humanity's future — the irreversible loss of what people value — not mere damage, disruption, or a
recoverable mistake.

The claim is conditional on superintelligence actually arriving. Whether such a system is possible at
all ([superintelligence-physically-possible](superintelligence-physically-possible.md)) and whether any development trajectory in fact
reaches it ([superintelligence-feasibility](../questions/superintelligence-feasibility.md)) are separate disputes it does not settle; it
asserts nothing about the timeline or probability of that antecedent, only about what follows if the
antecedent holds. It is contested from two directions. Some reject the orthogonality that lets a
highly capable system have humanly-arbitrary goals in the first place, holding instead that
sufficient intelligence converges on humane ends ([intelligence-entails-goal-convergence](intelligence-entails-goal-convergence.md)).
Others grant the setup but expect correction-in-flight to be easy — monitoring, shutdown, or the
system's own corrigibility catching the error after deployment ([just-turn-it-off](../arguments/just-turn-it-off.md)) — so
that a misspecified goal need not run to catastrophe.
