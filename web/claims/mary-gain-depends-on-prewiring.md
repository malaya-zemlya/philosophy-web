What happens when Mary the colour scientist first sees red is not settled by the thought experiment
alone. It depends on an empirical fact about her: how much of the pathway from her retina to the rest
of her mind is wired before any colour experience, and how much is set by that experience.

At one extreme, the whole colour pathway is fixed in advance. Then her first sight of red puts her
into the very state that anyone else's sight of red puts them in, and her experience is no different
from that of a person who has seen red and blue all their life. Her colour system needs no training,
so the encounter installs nothing new in it. At the other extreme, some stages of the pathway are
shaped by experience and start in a state Mary does not know. Then what happens downstream of those
stages on first exposure is learning, and its details depend on how learning actually works in her
brain. Most realistic cases lie in between, with an innately structured early system feeding later
stages that are tuned by use.

The language-model case of [llmary](../concepts/llmary.md) sits at the second extreme, since a never-seen token's
representation starts as arbitrary noise ([unknown-token-selection-bound](unknown-token-selection-bound.md)). Mary is closer to the
first than the model is, which limits how far conclusions about the model carry over to her
([mary-analogous-to-llmary](mary-analogous-to-llmary.md)). The question is also not idle for the original thought
experiment. Daniel Dennett notes that assuming Mary's colour system is "still intact" after a lifetime
without colour is "a nontrivial empirical assumption, given what is known about the ready reassignment
of unused cortical resources" ([dennett-2005](../sources/dennett-2005.md)).

### In plain terms

Whether Mary learns anything when she first sees red depends on how her brain is built. If her colour
vision is entirely wired up from birth, seeing red for the first time would be just like it is for
everyone else, with nothing new to set up. If parts of it are only shaped by actually seeing colours,
then her first sight of red would start that shaping, and exactly what she gains would depend on how
the brain learns.
