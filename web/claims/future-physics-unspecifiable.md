No one can now say what entities and properties a future or completed physics will posit, so a
definition of "physical" in terms of that physics gives the word no content we can presently grasp.
This is the second premise of [hempels-dilemma](../arguments/hempels-dilemma.md), drawn from Hempel
([1980](../sources/hempel-1980.md)): for all we know, a future physics might posit irreducibly mental
quantities, in which case a physicalism defined by it would no longer exclude what we now call
dualism.

**Scope.** The claim is epistemic: it says we cannot specify the future theory's content, not that
the future theory will be strange. It is the step from this unspecifiability to the *emptiness* of a
future-physics physicalism that the dilemma's critics most often reject.

**Who denies it.** Few deny the premise itself. Wilson ([2006](../sources/wilson-2006.md)) and the
defenders of the *via negativa* (Montero & Papineau, [2005](../sources/montero-papineau-2005.md)) grant
that the details of future physics are unknown, but argue that one constraint can still be stated in
advance, namely that it posits nothing fundamentally mental ([no-fundamental-mentality-reply](../arguments/no-fundamental-mentality-reply.md)).

Distinct from [current-physics-incomplete](current-physics-incomplete.md), which is about present physics.
