Even if cognitive states and processes such as beliefs, memories and calculations can be partly
constituted by things outside the brain ([extended-mind](../concepts/extended-mind.md)), it does not follow that conscious
experience is. The physical machinery of consciousness may stay in the head while cognition spreads
into notebooks and devices. The claim is one of non-entailment: it says that extended consciousness
does not follow from extended cognition, not that extended consciousness is impossible.

The usual ground is a difference in the kind of access involved. Andy Clark argues that conscious
experience depends on fast, high-bandwidth integration of information, and the loops linking a brain
to its external resources are too slow and narrow to take part in it ([clark-2009](../sources/clark-2009.md)). David
Chalmers argues that conscious states require direct access to information, whereas external
resources are reached only through perception and action ([chalmers-2019](../sources/chalmers-2019.md)). A notebook entry
can therefore be one of Otto's standing beliefs without being part of anything he is experiencing.

The claim is denied by defenders of [extended-consciousness](../concepts/extended-consciousness.md). Karina Vold argues that the
parity reasoning that supports the extended mind supports extended consciousness too, and that
differences of speed and bandwidth are contingent features of today's external resources, not a
principled barrier ([vold-2015](../sources/vold-2015.md); [parity-argument-extended-consciousness](../arguments/parity-argument-extended-consciousness.md)); Hurley and Noë hold that the vehicles of experience extend
into tightly coupled perception and action ([hurley-1998](../sources/hurley-1998.md), [noe-2009](../sources/noe-2009.md)). If
Vold is right, a sufficiently fast, integrated coupling would remove the ground for denial. The
dispute bears directly on artificial systems, where the coupling between a model and its context is
neither as loose as a notebook nor as fixed as a skull ([cot-bears-access-signature](cot-bears-access-signature.md)).
