A person can be wrong, at the time and not merely in memory, about what *kind* of activity they are
engaged in, including which sense it belongs to. The experience presents the activity one way while
the activity's actual structure, as shown by what it produces, is of another kind.

### The worked case

Under the dissociative drug dextromethorphan (DXM), which reliably induces synaesthesia-like
experiences, a subject working in a digital audio workstation experienced themselves as composing
music, and heard the result as musically interesting. The saved file showed that they had drawn a
smiley face in the *piano roll*, the editor in which notes appear as rectangles on a grid of pitch
against time. The case is unusually clean because in a piano roll one act has two true descriptions:
every rectangle is at once a picture element and a note. The error concerned which description was
in control. The structure of the output (eyes, a curved mouth, symmetry) shows that visual form was
guiding the hand, while the experience presented the activity as following sound. The subject also
reports that on DXM it can be impossible to tell whether an experience is a sound or an image.

### Scope

The claim does not say the experience was not music-like. It may have been accurately so. It says
that how the activity felt and what was actually organising it came apart, and that the subject could
not tell. The decisive fact, what the activity was, is fixed by the external record, not by memory.

It is distinct from the findings of Richard Nisbett and Timothy Wilson that people confidently
misreport the *reasons* for their choices ([nisbett-wilson-1977](../sources/nisbett-wilson-1977.md);
[introspective-confabulation](../arguments/introspective-confabulation.md)), since it concerns the
kind and modality of an ongoing activity rather than its causes. It extends the general case for the
unreliability of introspection made by Eric Schwitzgebel ([schwitzgebel-2008](../sources/schwitzgebel-2008.md)) to the
coarsest feature of an experience, its sense modality.

### In plain terms

Someone on a drug that mixes up the senses thought they were writing music, and it sounded good to
them. Afterwards the file showed they had actually drawn a smiley face out of musical notes. They had
been doing something visual while experiencing it as something musical, and could not tell the
difference while it was happening.
