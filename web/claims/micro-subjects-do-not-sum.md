title: Distinct micro-subjects cannot sum into a further macro-subject

A plurality of distinct experiencing subjects — each with its own point of view and its own self-contained
phenomenal field — does not, merely by being arranged or related, constitute a further single subject whose
experience contains theirs. As William James put it, a hundred feelings, however shuffled together, do not
make a hundred-and-first feeling that includes them; each is its own whole [james-1890](../sources/james-1890.md). Sam Coleman
sharpens the point: the would-be composite subject's experience is not entailed by the experiences of its
parts, and would in fact *exclude* them, since each micro-subject's perspective is private to it
[coleman-2014](../sources/coleman-2014.md).

This is the engine of the [combination problem](../arguments/combination-problem.md) facing constitutive
panpsychism. If micro-subjects cannot sum, then macro-minds cannot be *constituted* by the experiential
properties of their micro-parts, and the central constitutive claim
([consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous](consciousness-fundamental-ubiquitous.md)) fails as stated. The claim is denied by constitutive
panpsychists who offer an account of *phenomenal bonding* on which micro-subjects do compose a
macro-subject [goff-2016](../sources/goff-2016.md), and by combinationists who hold that a whole can literally share the
experiences of its parts [roelofs-2019](../sources/roelofs-2019.md). [panqualityism](../positions/panqualityism.md), by contrast, accepts it.
Coleman develops panqualityism precisely because he thinks subjects cannot sum [coleman-2014](../sources/coleman-2014.md). The
view escapes the objection by denying a different premise, that there are subjects at the fundamental
level at all: if the micro-level holds only unexperienced qualities, the claim is true but has nothing
there to apply to. It is the mirror image of the *decombination* problem
facing [cosmopsychism](../positions/cosmopsychism.md), where the puzzle is instead how one cosmic subject divides into many.
