On this view, what an experience is like is fixed entirely by where it sits in the subject's mental
economy. That place is given by what brings the experience on, how it resembles and differs from
other experiences, and what it leads the subject to expect, recall, imagine, say and do. The redness
of red is not a quality the experience has in itself, apart from these relations. It *is* its place
among them. Change the relations enough and the quality changes. Strip them away and nothing
determinate is left, not even which sense the experience belongs to.

### Scope

The claim accepts qualia in the *thin* sense of [qualia](../concepts/qualia.md): experiences really do feel some
way, and the claim does not deny it. What it rejects is the *thick* sense, on which those feels are
intrinsic properties known directly by introspection. It is therefore a reconstruction of qualia,
not an elimination of them, and is distinct from Daniel Dennett's verdict in "Quining Qualia" that
qualia should be abandoned ([dennett-1988](../sources/dennett-1988.md)). It is also distinct from:

- [phenomenal-character-is-representational](phenomenal-character-is-representational.md), Michael Tye's view that phenomenal character is
  the content an experience represents. That too makes character relational, but relational to the
  *world* represented, whereas this claim makes it relational to the subject's *own* states and
  dispositions.
- [phenomenality-is-illusion](phenomenality-is-illusion.md), Keith Frankish's illusionism, on which phenomenal consciousness as
  standardly conceived is an introspective illusion ([illusionism](../positions/illusionism.md)). Illusionism denies the thin
  qualia this claim accepts, or reinterprets them as mere seemings; this claim holds that experiences
  really feel some way, and locates the feel in relations.
- Austen Clark's quality-space account, on which sensory qualities are individuated by their
  relations of similarity and difference ([clark-1993](../sources/clark-1993.md)). This claim is close kin, but
  broader. It counts not only similarity relations but also a state's typical causes and
  consequences as constitutive of its character.

### Who would deny it

This claim denies a thesis that is a basic building block of many theories of consciousness:
[consciousness-is-intrinsic-property](consciousness-is-intrinsic-property.md), the thesis that experience is a paradigm of what a
thing is in itself, the kind of property a duplicate would keep even alone in an empty universe. Its
companions are [consciousness-only-known-intrinsic](consciousness-only-known-intrinsic.md) (introspection acquaints us with an
intrinsic nature) and [phenomenal-residue](phenomenal-states-not-exhausted-by-role.md) (a complete specification of
what a state does leaves its feel out). Theories that rest on one or more of these include:

- the [intrinsic-nature](../arguments/intrinsic-nature.md), which takes experience to be the hidden intrinsic nature of
  matter, and the positions it supports: [russellian-monism](../positions/russellian-monism.md), [panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md),
  [panqualityism](../positions/panqualityism.md) and [cosmopsychism](../positions/cosmopsychism.md);
- [dualism](../positions/dualism.md), on which phenomenal properties are something over and above any physical or
  functional relations;
- the anti-physicalist thought experiments that hold the relations fixed while varying the feel:
  [zombie-conceivability](../arguments/zombie-conceivability.md) (same functional relations, no feel at all),
  [china-nation-absent-qualia](../arguments/china-nation-absent-qualia.md) and the inverted spectrum, and the
  [knowledge-argument](../arguments/knowledge-argument.md) (complete relational knowledge, yet something left out).

If qualia are wholly relational, each of these loses a premise. A zombie or spectrum-invert would be
impossible, not just unlikely, since a duplicate of all the relations would be a duplicate of the
feel.

The claim also sits uneasily with [phenomenal-determinacy](phenomenal-determinacy.md), the thesis that there is always a
determinate fact about one's current phenomenal state. If the relations that fix a quality can be stripped
away or scrambled, as the evidence suggests, the quality can become indeterminate.

The evidence for the claim is gathered in [qualia-relational-from-cases](../arguments/qualia-relational-from-cases.md).

### In plain terms

Many philosophers think the redness of red is a quality an experience has *in itself*, which would
stay exactly the same even if everything around it changed. This claim says the opposite: the way red
looks to you just *is* how that experience fits with everything else in your mind, such as what
causes it, what it resembles, and what it makes you expect or do. There is nothing left over once you
take all of that away. If that is right, several famous arguments that consciousness can't be
explained scientifically lose their footing.
