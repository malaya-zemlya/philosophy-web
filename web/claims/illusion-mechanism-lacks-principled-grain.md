Illusionism explains the appearance of phenomenal consciousness by an introspective mechanism that
misrepresents the system's own states. The claim is that the specifications of that mechanism
currently on offer fall into two groups. Strict ones, which require explicit metacognition, leave
out creatures that most people, illusionists included, count as conscious. Loose ones, which require
only self-monitoring, feedback or global availability of information, also take in thermostats,
homeostatic loops and power grids. The claim does not say that no principled middle exists, only
that none has been given. It is argued for in [illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma](../arguments/illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma.md), and it
applies to illusionism the general problem of grain ([grain](../concepts/grain.md)).
