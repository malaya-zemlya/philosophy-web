Mary the colour scientist on her release and a language model meeting a token for the first time are
alike in the respects that matter for what each gains. Each has complete descriptive knowledge of the
item beforehand. Each has never had the item itself occur in its own processing. Each adjusts its
internal states when the item finally does occur.

### Scope

The claim asserts a *relevant* similarity: that whatever structure the model's gain has, Mary's gain
shares it. It does not assert that Mary is a language model, that the model has experiences, or that
the two systems are alike in every respect. It is distinct from the setup of
[llmary-token-encounter](../arguments/llmary-token-encounter.md), which needs only that LLMary instantiates the knowledge
argument's antecedent. This claim is stronger, because it carries over the *content* of the gain and
not merely the fact that some gain occurs.

### Who would deny it

The phenomenal realist denies it. On that view the model's update is purely a change in information
processing, while Mary's gain includes acquaintance with what red is like, so the two cases differ in
exactly the respect at issue ([mary-gains-acquaintance](mary-gains-acquaintance.md)). Biological naturalists deny it on
broader grounds, holding that a digital system's state changes are not the right kind of thing to
model a conscious subject's learning.

### In plain terms

The idea is that Mary seeing red for the first time is basically the same kind of event as an AI
meeting a new word-piece for the first time: both knew all about it in advance, both finally meet it,
and both adjust. Critics say the cases differ in the one way that matters, since Mary *feels*
something and the AI does not.
