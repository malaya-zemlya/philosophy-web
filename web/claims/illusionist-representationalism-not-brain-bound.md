On illusionism, the felt specialness of experience is an illusion and what is real is a kind of
representation. If so, consciousness in the only sense the illusionist accepts reaches as far as
representation does. On a notion of representation satisfied by causal dependence, that is every
physical system ([causal-dependence-representation-trivial](causal-dependence-representation-trivial.md)), a deflationary panpsychism. On
the restricted notions in the literature it is at least single-celled organisms or feedback
controllers ([use-criteria-regress](use-criteria-regress.md)), a form of biopsychism. Either way, the slogan applies:
on illusionism, the difference between a person and a photographic plate, or a bacterium, is that the
person is deceived about themself.

The claim is about *distribution*, not nature. The panpsychism it yields lacks the intrinsic
phenomenal properties that realist panpsychism ([panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md)) asserts. It is denied by
illusionists who hold that a further, non-representational criterion confines consciousness to
creatures with brains of a certain kind; on that reply see [illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma](../arguments/illusion-mechanism-grain-dilemma.md).

### In plain terms

If consciousness is really nothing but the brain representing things, and the rest is a trick the
brain plays on itself, then anything else that represents has the real part too. It just isn't fooled
about it. Since a great many things represent, at least in a modest sense, consciousness on this view
spreads far beyond brains.
