A world of zombies, beings that behave much as we do but have no experience, could arrive at
science and mathematics like ours. It could not arrive at some of our concepts of the mind. Its
members could treat dreaming only as acquiring, on waking, apparent memories that do not fit,
because they have no notion of a dream as something experienced. The inverted spectrum would mean
nothing to them, since it depends on a notion of "internal seeing", and neither would the
other-minds problem. The *Terminator* films' view through the robot's eyes, with readouts across the
bottom of the screen, makes sense to us only because we can imagine seeing such readouts internally
([moody-1994](../sources/moody-1994.md)). Todd Moody calls finding such gaps a kind of hermeneutic analysis: the
difference between zombies and us shows in what each community talks about, not in what their
natural science finds.

The claim concerns where concepts come from, not whether they can be used. A zombie raised among
conscious speakers could become fluent in their talk of experience
([inherited-consciousness-talk-not-evidence](inherited-consciousness-talk-not-evidence.md)).

### In plain terms

Beings with no inner experience could copy our talk about dreams, feelings and what colours look
like if they learned it from us. Left to themselves, though, they would never have invented that talk,
because there would have been nothing for it to be about.
