Igor Mikhailov counts falsifiability among the criteria for a good empirical theory of consciousness
([Mikhailov, 2024](../sources/mikhailov-2024.md)). Vadim Vasilyev replies that, applied without care, the
criterion admits too much, so that any thesis could turn out to be falsifiable
([Vasilyev, 2024](../sources/vasilyev-2024.md)). He proposes instead the
requirement of *weak direct verifiability*: a theory's claims should be directly confirmable, at least
in a weak sense. On this criterion a theory of consciousness is *speculative* when it contains claims
that cannot be directly verified.

The claim concerns the demarcation of empirical theories of consciousness, not the theory of
confirmation in general. It leaves room for non-experimental theories, a point taken up in
[armchair-theories-need-not-be-speculative](armchair-theories-need-not-be-speculative.md).
