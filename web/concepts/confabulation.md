*Confabulation* is the sincere production of a false or ill-grounded account, typically about one's
own memories or the causes of one's own behaviour, by a person who does not realise that the account
is unfounded. The confabulator is not lying: the invented story is offered fluently and confidently,
and from the inside it feels exactly like remembering or knowing.

### Clinical and everyday senses

The term comes from neurology, where it describes patients who fill gaps in memory with fabricated
episodes. It is characteristic of Korsakoff's syndrome and of some kinds of damage to the frontal
lobes. Related clinical forms include the denial of an obvious disability (a paralysed patient who
insists that the limb moves) and the explanations offered by split-brain patients for actions
initiated by the hemisphere their speech centre has no access to ([gazzaniga-1970](../sources/gazzaniga-1970.md)). The
philosopher William Hirstein argues that these syndromes share a failure of the brain's checking
processes, so that a candidate answer is endorsed without being tested, and he defines confabulating
as sincerely making an ill-grounded claim without knowing that it is ill-grounded
([hirstein-2005](../sources/hirstein-2005.md)).

In a broader sense the word is applied to healthy people. In the experiments surveyed by Richard
Nisbett and Timothy Wilson, shoppers asked to choose among four identical pairs of stockings tended to
pick the rightmost pair, then explained their choice by its superior knit or sheerness, never
mentioning its position. Asked directly whether position mattered, they denied it
([nisbett-wilson-1977](../sources/nisbett-wilson-1977.md)). On Nisbett and Wilson's interpretation, people generally lack direct
access to the processes behind their judgments and generate explanations from plausible theories of
why someone would act that way. Where the theory matches the actual cause, the report is right; where
it does not, the result is confabulation ([introspective-confabulation](../arguments/introspective-confabulation.md)).

### Neighbouring phenomena

Confabulation is one of several ways a self-report can come apart from what actually happened, and it
is useful to keep them apart. In [hindsight bias](hindsight-bias.md) people misremember the content of their own earlier
beliefs, recalling that they "knew all along" an outcome they had in fact doubted. That is a
reconstruction of past belief, where confabulation in Nisbett and Wilson's sense is a reconstruction of
present causes. In [incubation](incubation.md) a solution arrives with its origin unknown, and the person who says
"it just came to me" gives an account that is incomplete but true. The difference between leaving
out what drove a result and misdescribing what drove it is what separates incubation from
confabulation ([omission-is-not-confabulation](../claims/omission-is-not-confabulation.md)).

### Significance

Confabulation is the main empirical evidence against the view that introspection gives direct,
infallible access to one's own mind, and for the rival view that self-reports are constructed
representations that can be wrong ([introspection-is-fallible-representation](../claims/introspection-is-fallible-representation.md)). It has
become pressing again with large language models, whose written chains of reasoning can omit the
factor that actually determined an answer while offering a different, plausible rationale
([chen-2025-cot](../sources/chen-2025-cot.md)). Whether such cases are best described as confabulation is part of the wider
question of whether these systems can introspect at all ([ai-introspection](../questions/ai-introspection.md)).

### In plain terms

To confabulate is to tell a made-up story about yourself without knowing it is made up. Brain-injured
patients do it to fill holes in memory, but healthy people do it too: in one famous study, shoppers
picked the right-hand pair of identical stockings and then praised its quality, never realising that
the position was what swayed them. The point is that we often don't know why we do things, and our
minds fill in a believable reason that feels like knowledge.
