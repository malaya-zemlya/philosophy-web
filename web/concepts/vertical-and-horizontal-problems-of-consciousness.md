Matvey Sysoev proposes classifying problems of consciousness by the *relation* each one asks to have
explained ([Sysoev, 2025](../sources/sysoev-2025.md)). He distinguishes three levels of description of
consciousness ([level-of-abstraction-conflation](level-of-abstraction-conflation.md)), each with its own kinds of element:

| Level | Whole element | Specific elements |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenological | the overall conscious state | specific conscious states |
| Cognitive | overall representational content | representations and features |
| Neurobiological | the brain | brain areas |

A relation is *intra-type* when it holds between elements of the same level and *inter-type* when it
crosses levels. It is *vertical* when it holds between a whole element and specific elements, and
*horizontal* when it holds only between wholes or only between specific elements.

### Placing the classic problems

The most discussed part of the mind-body problem concerns *inter-type horizontal* relations: between
the overall conscious state and the brain, between specific conscious states and brain areas, between
specific conscious states and representations, and between representations and brain areas. The
search for [neural correlates](neural-correlates-of-consciousness.md) is this problem pursued
under the constraints of empirical method, settling for correlation, the weakest relation on which
scientific conclusions rest. The relation between specific conscious states and representations,
between the phenomenal and the representational, forms the core of the hard problem.

The problems of [unity](unity-of-consciousness.md) and [binding](binding-problem.md)
concern *vertical* relations, how many specific states, representations or brain processes make up
one whole. Some of these are intra-type: between the overall conscious state and its parts, between a
represented object and its features, and between the brain and its parts. Others cross levels.
Following Tim Bayne and David Chalmers ([Bayne & Chalmers, 2003](../sources/bayne-chalmers-2003.md)), Sysoev
treats the binding problem, insofar as it bears on the nature of consciousness, as the
cognitive-neurobiological side of the problem of unity.

The classification grounds the claim that these problems cannot be studied in isolation
([mind-body-problem-inseparable-from-unity-and-binding](../claims/mind-body-problem-inseparable-from-unity-and-binding.md)).

### In plain terms

Questions about consciousness can be sorted by what they ask to connect. Some ask how two things at
different levels are linked, such as a feeling and a brain area. Others ask how many parts make up one
whole, such as how separate sights and sounds make up one experience. The first kind is the classic
mind-body problem. The second kind is the problem of unity. This classification makes it easier to see
how the problems depend on each other.
