*Superintelligence* is, in Nick Bostrom's canonical definition, "an intellect that greatly
exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest"
([bostrom-2014](../sources/bostrom-2014.md)). The key words are *greatly*, *virtually all*, and *domains of
interest*: the term is reserved not for a system that beats us at one task, or even at many, but
for one that outstrips the best human minds across essentially the whole range of thinking that
matters — scientific discovery, strategic planning, social manipulation, engineering, and more.
It is a notion of degree and breadth together, marking a level of capability decisively above the
human ceiling.

### The ancestor notion: Good's ultraintelligent machine

The idea has a clear ancestor in the mathematician I. J. Good, who in 1965 imagined an
*ultraintelligent machine*: "a machine that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of
any man however clever" ([good-1966](../sources/good-1966.md)). Good's phrase already carries the two features
Bostrom would sharpen — surpassing the best of us, and doing so across all intellectual activity
rather than in a single skill. Bostrom's definition can be read as a careful restatement of
Good's, trading the older "ultraintelligent" for "superintelligent" and pinning down the vague
"far surpass" as *greatly exceeds ... in virtually all domains*.

### Bostrom's taxonomy

Bostrom notes that a system might tower over human intelligence in more than one way, and he
distinguishes three forms a superintelligence could take ([bostrom-2014](../sources/bostrom-2014.md)):

- **Speed superintelligence** — a mind that does what a human mind does, only much faster. A
  human-equivalent intellect running thousands of times quicker could accomplish in an afternoon
  what a human researcher would take years to finish; the qualitative repertoire is human, the
  clock is not.
- **Collective superintelligence** — a large number of smaller intellects networked and
  coordinated so that the system as a whole vastly outperforms any single mind. The analogy is to
  what organizations, markets, or scientific communities already achieve, pushed to a degree at
  which the collective's problem-solving power dwarfs any individual's.
- **Quality superintelligence** — a mind at least as fast as a human's but *qualitatively*
  smarter, in the way human intelligence is qualitatively superior to that of an elephant or a
  chimpanzee. This is the hardest form to picture from the inside, because it posits kinds of
  insight and understanding no human could reach at any speed or in any number.

The three are not exclusive; a real system might combine them. They matter because they show that
"superintelligence" names a *level* of performance that can be reached by different routes, not a
single architecture.

### A performance notion, not a mind

The definition is deliberately silent about the inner life of the system. Superintelligence does
**not** require consciousness, human-like motivation or emotion, or an embodied form: it is a
claim about what a system can *do*, not about what — if anything — it is like to be that system.
It is defined over the *instrumental* sense of [intelligence](intelligence.md) — intelligence as
effective, cross-domain problem-solving and goal-achievement — and takes no stand on the felt or
phenomenal side. A superintelligence in this sense could in principle be an entirely unconscious
optimizer.

Two contrasts fix the idea. Today's chess and Go programs are radically superhuman, yet only at
one game apiece; they are examples of *narrow* superhuman skill, not superintelligence, precisely
because their mastery does not generalize past the board. Superintelligence is the general,
all-domains notion — the difference the term is built to mark. And because the definition speaks
of exceeding humans "in virtually all domains," it presupposes that intelligence can be *compared
across domains* and read off on a single scale. That presupposition is not innocent: the
multidimensional view of intelligence holds that cognitive abilities are too heterogeneous to
collapse onto one axis ([intelligence-not-one-dimensional](../claims/intelligence-not-one-dimensional.md)), which would make "greatly
exceeds in virtually all domains" harder to state cleanly. Whether the notion is coherent and
achievable in this light is not settled here; that dispute lives at
[superintelligence-feasibility](../questions/superintelligence-feasibility.md).
