The *conflation of levels of abstraction* is the error of treating an analysis of consciousness at one
level of description as if it were an analysis at another. Matvey Sysoev names it, alongside the
[mereological-fallacy](mereological-fallacy.md), as a methodological obstacle in consciousness research
([Sysoev, 2025](../sources/sysoev-2025.md)). An abstraction picks out some properties of a phenomenon and
sets others aside, as when a bird's flight is treated simply as the motion of a body along a
trajectory. A level of abstraction is the set of properties that can be considered together.

Following Antti Revonsuo ([Revonsuo, 1999](../sources/revonsuo-1999.md)), Sysoev uses three levels. At the
*phenomenological* level consciousness is described from the first person. At the *cognitive* level it
is a function realized by operations on representations. At the *neurobiological* level it is a
biological object, the brain, and its processes. To claim to have explained consciousness by
considering only the cognitive or only the neurobiological level is an instance of the error. As with
the mereological fallacy, Sysoev presses the charge in a weak sense: the mistake is to assume without
grounds that one level exhausts consciousness, which silently answers a question that should be
argued.

Each level has its own elements, and the relations among them are what problems of consciousness ask
to have explained ([vertical-and-horizontal-problems-of-consciousness](vertical-and-horizontal-problems-of-consciousness.md)).

### In plain terms

You can describe consciousness as it feels from the inside, as information processing, or as brain
activity. Each description leaves things out. The mistake is to explain consciousness in one of these
terms, say as brain activity, and then assume the other descriptions have been taken care of too.
