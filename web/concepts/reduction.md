*Reduction* is the relation that holds when one thing is shown to be nothing over and above another:
when a theory, a kind or a property is explained by, and in the end identified with, something more
basic. The textbook cases come from physics and chemistry. Temperature in a gas turned out to be the
mean kinetic energy of its molecules, and water turned out to be H₂O. After such a reduction there
are not two things, the temperature and the molecular motion, but one thing described in two
vocabularies. In the philosophy of mind the question is whether mental states will turn out the same
way, as nothing over and above states of the brain.

### Models of reduction

The classical model is Ernest Nagel's ([nagel-1961](../sources/nagel-1961.md)). A theory reduces to another when its
laws can be derived from the more basic theory together with *bridge laws* that connect the two
vocabularies, such as a law pairing each temperature with a mean molecular energy. On this model,
reducing psychology to neuroscience would require bridge laws linking each mental kind to a
neural kind. The [type-identity](../positions/type-identity.md) theory supplied exactly that, in its strongest form, by
holding that each mental type simply *is* a brain-state type ([mental-states-are-brain-state-types](../claims/mental-states-are-brain-state-types.md)).

A later model, developed by Jaegwon Kim, is *functional reduction* ([kim-2005](../sources/kim-2005.md)). First a
property is defined by its causal role. Being a gene, for instance, is being whatever carries
hereditary information and passes it from parent to offspring. Then science finds what plays that
role, in this case DNA. On this model a mental state reduces if it can be defined by its role and the
physical occupant of the role can be found. Kim argued that most mental properties, such as belief
and desire, can be reduced this way, but that the felt qualities of experience cannot, since they do
not seem to be definable by any causal role.

### Reductive and non-reductive physicalism

*Reductive physicalism* holds that mental properties are identical to, or reducible to, physical
properties. *Non-reductive physicalism* holds that everything is physical but that mental properties
are not identical to physical ones. They are realized by physical properties and
[supervene](supervenience.md) on them, without reducing to them. The main reason for the
non-reductive view is [multiple-realizability](multiple-realizability.md): if pain can be realized in physically very
different systems, no single physical type is available to identify it with, and the laws of
psychology have no counterpart among the laws of physics ([fodor-1974](../sources/fodor-1974.md)). Donald Davidson's
[anomalous-monism](../positions/anomalous-monism.md) is a non-reductive view of a different kind, resting on the claim that
there are no strict psychophysical laws at all ([no-strict-psychophysical-laws](../claims/no-strict-psychophysical-laws.md)).

The main challenge to non-reductive physicalism concerns causation. If a mental property is not
identical to its physical base, and the physical base is already sufficient to cause behaviour, then
the mental property seems to have nothing left to do. Kim's [causal-exclusion](../arguments/causal-exclusion.md) presses this
as a dilemma: either mental properties reduce, or they are causally idle
([irreducible-mental-causally-excluded](../claims/irreducible-mental-causally-excluded.md)).

### Reduction and explanation

A separate question is whether reduction must be *explanatory*. Identifying water with H₂O comes with
an account of why H₂O behaves as water does, boiling, dissolving and freezing as it should. An
identification of pain with a brain state seems to leave open why that state should feel like
anything at all. This is the [explanatory-gap](explanatory-gap.md), and it is why some philosophers accept a
physicalism about experience while denying that experience can be reduced in the explanatory sense.

### In plain terms

To reduce one thing to another is to show that the first is really just the second, described
differently. Scientists showed that the temperature of a gas is just how fast its molecules are
moving, and that water is just H₂O. Reductive physicalists think the mind will go the same way, with
every thought and feeling turning out to be some brain process. Non-reductive physicalists agree that
the mind is entirely physical but deny that each mental state matches one kind of brain state, partly
because quite different brains, or even machines, might share the same mental states.
