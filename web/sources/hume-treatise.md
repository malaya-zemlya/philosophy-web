relevance (content): the source of the Humean theory of motivation — reason discovers means and
traces consequences but originates no ends of its own, so a passion (a desire, an aim) can never
be opposed or corrected by reason alone. The thesis is compressed in the famous line "Reason is,
and ought only to be the slave of the passions" (Book 2, Part 3, Section 3, "Of the influencing
motives of the will"; SBN p. 415). Grounds the Humean theory of motivation invoked by
[humean-defense-of-orthogonality](../arguments/humean-defense-of-orthogonality.md) and presupposed by [orthogonality-thesis](../claims/orthogonality-thesis.md).

Reference:
Hume, D. (1978). *A treatise of human nature* (L. A. Selby-Bigge & P. H. Nidditch, Eds.; 2nd ed.). Clarendon Press. (Original work published 1739–1740)
