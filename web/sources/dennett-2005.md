relevance (content): the source of the **RoboMary** counter-image — a robot that knows the complete
functional story of its own colour vision can compute and pre-instantiate the total state seeing red
would induce, meeting its first red object with no update. The constructive counter-case used in
[mary-misimagined](../arguments/mary-misimagined.md) and in the hard-line reply [mary-learns-nothing-new](../claims/mary-learns-nothing-new.md). RoboMary
appears in the chapter "What RoboMary Knows." Distinct from [dennett-1991](dennett-1991.md) (*Consciousness
Explained*).

relevance (content): the chapter's later sections supply the self-modification cases of
[self-access-kinds](../concepts/self-access-kinds.md). §2, "'Surely' she'll be surprised", replies to Graham and Horgan
([graham-horgan-2000](graham-horgan-2000.md)) and holds that a Mary with all the physical facts would neither expect
nor undergo surprise. §3 introduces Swamp Mary, whose brain a lightning bolt puts into "the
dispositional state, the competence state" that seeing a red rose would have produced, and notes that
Mary's colour system being "still intact" after confinement is "a nontrivial empirical assumption".
§5, "Locked RoboMary", has a robot whose colour system is locked compute, with her self-model, the
state she would have entered and then put herself into it, so that "there are no surprises for her".
These ground [write-access-installs-binding](../claims/write-access-installs-binding.md) and [mary-gain-depends-on-prewiring](../claims/mary-gain-depends-on-prewiring.md).

Reference:
- Dennett, D. C. (2005). What RoboMary knows. In *Sweet dreams: Philosophical obstacles to a science of
  consciousness* (pp. 103–130). MIT Press.
