relevance (content): the origin of the *intermediate-level theory* of consciousness. Jackendoff
distinguishes the computational from the phenomenological mind and names the relation between them the
*mind-mind problem* (ch. 2, pp. 18–19), adopting as ground rules the Hypotheses of Computational
Sufficiency and of the Nonefficacy of Consciousness (pp. 24–25). Surveying the levels of representation
posited for language, music and vision, he argues (ch. 14, pp. 285–301) that the form of awareness in
each modality is projected from an intermediate level: phonological structure for language (Theory VII,
preliminary form, p. 289), the musical surface for music, and Marr's 2½-D sketch for vision, with
syntax, conceptual structure, the higher musical hierarchies and the 3-D model unconscious. The general
statement of Theory VII is on p. 298; ch. 15 adds the *affects* (outer/inner, ego-initiated, meaningful,
familiar) as the source of the "feel" of experience (Theory VIII, p. 310). Grounds
[intermediate-level-theory](../positions/intermediate-level-theory.md) and [consciousness-is-intermediate-level](../claims/consciousness-is-intermediate-level.md). A copy and
its text conversion are kept in `external/` (`jackendoff-1987/`).

Reference:
- Jackendoff, R. (1987). *Consciousness and the computational mind*. MIT Press.
