relevance (content): develops Feigl's "autocerebroscope", a device that shows a subject the current
state of his own visual cortex, into a full thought experiment. The subject, a neurophysiologist,
finds that on about one trial in ten the device reads the brain state for red while he is certain he
sees green. Meehl argues that a well-confirmed theory could rationally lead him to conclude that his
report of his own experience is the thing in error, and that raw-feel statements are normally
noninferential and indubitable but corrigible and sometimes wrong. He also argues that the identity
thesis is empirical, since imaginable results of such experiments could tell against it. Cited in
[consciousness-reading-machine](../questions/consciousness-reading-machine.md). A copy is kept in `external/meehl/`.

Reference:
- Meehl, P. E. (1966). The compleat autocerebroscopist: A thought-experiment on Professor Feigl's
  mind-body identity thesis. In P. K. Feyerabend & G. Maxwell (Eds.), *Mind, matter, and method:
  Essays in philosophy and science in honor of Herbert Feigl* (pp. 103–180). University of Minnesota
  Press.
