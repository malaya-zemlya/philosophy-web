relevance (content): the report of a Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists working party (2008–2009), revising the College's 1997 report. It concludes that the connections from the periphery to the cortex, routed through the thalamus, are not intact before 24 weeks of gestation, so the fetus cannot experience pain before then and fetal analgesia is not recommended before that point. It adds that even after 24 weeks the fetus appears to be kept in a continuous sleep-like, sedated state by its chemical environment in utero, which makes awareness unlikely before birth. Grounds the 24-week and birth thresholds discussed in [onset-of-consciousness](../questions/onset-of-consciousness.md).

Reference:
- Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists. (2010). *Fetal awareness: Review of research and recommendations for practice* (Report of a working party). RCOG.
