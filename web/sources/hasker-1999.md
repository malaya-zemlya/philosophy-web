relevance (content): Hasker's emergent dualism: the mind or soul is a new individual substance that emerges from the brain when it is suitably organised, sustained by it but not reducible to it. He argues, against materialism, that the unity of consciousness cannot belong to a composite physical system, and, against Cartesian dualism, that the soul is a natural product rather than separately created. Grounds the emergentist option in [soul](../concepts/soul.md) and [soul](../questions/soul.md), as a middle way between [soul-is-immaterial-substance](../claims/soul-is-immaterial-substance.md) and [christian-physicalism](../positions/christian-physicalism.md).

Reference:
- Hasker, W. (1999). *The emergent self*. Cornell University Press.
