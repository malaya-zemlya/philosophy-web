relevance (content): the leading statement of nonreductive physicalism as a Christian anthropology. Murphy argues that biblical talk of soul and spirit is best read as describing aspects of a unified person rather than separable parts, that neuroscience has taken over the functions once assigned to the soul, and that resurrection rather than soul survival is the Christian hope; she also argues that physicalism need not be reductive. Grounds [christian-physicalism](../positions/christian-physicalism.md) and its answer to [soul](../questions/soul.md).

Reference:
- Murphy, N. (2006). *Bodies and souls, or spirited bodies?* (Current Issues in Theology). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511802805
