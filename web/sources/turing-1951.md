relevance (content): a lecture (delivered to the '51 Society, broadcast on the BBC Home Service)
in which Turing already voices — and doubts — the hope of retaining control over a thinking
machine "by turning off the power at strategic moments." He writes: "Even if we could keep the
machines in a subservient position, for instance by turning off the power at strategic moments, we
should, as a species, feel greatly humbled." The remark is an early statement of the off-switch
hope behind [just-turn-it-off](../arguments/just-turn-it-off.md), and Turing's unease already anticipates its limits.

Reference:
- Turing, A. M. (2004). Intelligent machinery, a heretical theory. In B. J. Copeland (Ed.), *The
  essential Turing: Seminal writings in computing, logic, philosophy, artificial intelligence, and
  artificial life* (pp. 465–475). Oxford University Press. (Original work delivered c. 1951)
