relevance (content): the paper that distinguished the main varieties of supervenience (weak, strong
and global) and asked which of them, if any, expresses a genuine relation of dependence. Kim argues
that weak and global supervenience are too weak to guarantee dependency, which became the standard
starting point for later debate. Grounds the senses of the term in [supervenience](../concepts/supervenience.md).
Distinct from [kim-2005](kim-2005.md), which uses supervenience in the causal exclusion argument.

Reference:
- Kim, J. (1984). Concepts of supervenience. *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*, *45*(2),
  153–176. (Reprinted in Kim, J. (1993). *Supervenience and mind: Selected philosophical essays*
  (pp. 53–78). Cambridge University Press.)
