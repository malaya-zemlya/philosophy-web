relevance (content): the classic statement of British emergentism. Broad distinguishes "mechanistic"
from "emergent" theories of a whole's behaviour: on an emergent theory, the properties of a complex
could not be deduced even from complete knowledge of its parts and of how those parts behave in
other combinations, so further "trans-ordinal" laws are needed to link the levels. Grounds the
historical sense of strong emergence in [emergence](../concepts/emergence.md).

Reference:
- Broad, C. D. (1925). *The mind and its place in nature*. Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner.
