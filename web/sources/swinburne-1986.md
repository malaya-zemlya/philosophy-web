relevance (content): the leading contemporary defence of Cartesian substance dualism. Swinburne argues that mental events are not physical events, that personal identity is unanalysable (no bodily or psychological criterion fixes it), and, by a modal argument, that a person can exist without a body and so has an immaterial soul as an essential part; he also discusses the soul's evolutionary origin and the prospects of survival. Grounds [soul-is-immaterial-substance](../claims/soul-is-immaterial-substance.md) and the dualist answer in [soul](../questions/soul.md) and [soul](../concepts/soul.md). A revised edition with new appendices appeared in 1997.

Reference:
- Swinburne, R. (1986). *The evolution of the soul*. Clarendon Press.
