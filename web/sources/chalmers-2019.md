relevance (content): Chalmers asks what exactly the extended mind thesis claims and whether there
are any cases of extended consciousness. He argues that there are not, on the ground that conscious
states require a kind of direct access to information that external resources, reached only through
perception and action, do not provide. This develops a suggestion in his foreword to Clark's
*Supersizing the Mind* (2008) that our low-bandwidth connection to the environment has the wrong
form to support consciousness. Cited in [extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness](../claims/extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness.md).

Reference:
- Chalmers, D. J. (2019). Extended cognition and extended consciousness. In M. Colombo, E. Irvine, & M. Stapleton (Eds.), *Andy Clark and his critics* (pp. 9–20). Oxford University Press.
