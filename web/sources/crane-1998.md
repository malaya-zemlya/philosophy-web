relevance (content): a defence of Brentano's thesis that all and only mental phenomena are intentional, against the widespread view that bodily sensations and undirected moods are counterexamples. Crane argues that pains and other sensations have intentional objects (parts of the body, represented in a certain way), so intentionality can serve as the mark of the mental. Grounds the intentionalist reply in [mark-of-the-mental](../concepts/mark-of-the-mental.md).

Reference:
- Crane, T. (1998). Intentionality as the mark of the mental. In A. O'Hear (Ed.), *Current issues in philosophy of mind* (Royal Institute of Philosophy Supplement 43, pp. 229–252). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511563744.013
