relevance (content): the founding text of Western soul–body dualism. On his last day Socrates argues that the soul is distinct from the body, that the philosopher's life is a practice for death, and that the soul is immortal, through the cyclical argument, the argument from recollection, the affinity argument (the soul resembles the invisible, unchanging Forms), and the final argument from the soul's essential participation in life. Grounds the Platonic strand of [soul](../concepts/soul.md), [soul](../questions/soul.md), and [soul-is-immaterial-substance](../claims/soul-is-immaterial-substance.md).

Reference:
- Plato. (1997). Phaedo (G. M. A. Grube, Trans.). In J. M. Cooper & D. S. Hutchinson (Eds.), *Plato: Complete works* (pp. 49–100). Hackett.
