relevance (content): argues that the primary integrative system for conscious experience lies in the upper brainstem and diencephalon rather than the cerebral cortex. Its central evidence is children with hydranencephaly, born with little or no cortex, who nonetheless show wakefulness, emotional responsiveness, and orientation to their surroundings. If correct, the cortical and thalamocortical thresholds used to date the onset of consciousness are too late, a point that bears on [onset-of-consciousness](../questions/onset-of-consciousness.md).

Reference:
- Merker, B. (2007). Consciousness without a cerebral cortex: A challenge for neuroscience and medicine. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, *30*(1), 63–81. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X07000891
