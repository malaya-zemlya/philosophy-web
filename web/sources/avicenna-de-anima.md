relevance (content): the psychology of Avicenna's *Book of the Healing*, his fullest treatment of the soul. The floating (or flying) man appears at I.1 and again at V.7: a man created fully formed, suspended in air, with sight veiled and limbs apart so that he senses nothing, not even his own body, would still affirm his own existence, so the self he is aware of is not the body or any bodily part. Book V also argues that the rational soul's intellection cannot occur in a bodily organ, so the soul is an immaterial substance that does not perish with the body. Grounds [floating-man](../arguments/floating-man.md) and [soul-is-immaterial-substance](../claims/soul-is-immaterial-substance.md). Rahman's is the standard edition of the Arabic text.

Reference:
- Avicenna. (1959). *Avicenna's De anima (Arabic text): Being the psychological part of Kitāb al-Shifāʾ* (F. Rahman, Ed.). Oxford University Press.
