relevance (content): distinguishes several notions of the unity of consciousness and identifies
phenomenal unity, the co-subsumption of experiences within a single total experience, as the central
one, stating the unity thesis that Bayne later defends at length. Cited in
[unity-of-consciousness](../concepts/unity-of-consciousness.md).

Reference:
- Bayne, T., & Chalmers, D. J. (2003). What is the unity of consciousness? In A. Cleeremans (Ed.),
  *The unity of consciousness: Binding, integration, and dissociation* (pp. 23–58). Oxford
  University Press.
