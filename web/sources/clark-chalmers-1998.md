relevance (content): the paper that launched the extended mind thesis. Clark and Chalmers propose a
*parity principle* (if a process in the world plays the role that would count as cognitive were it
done in the head, it is part of the cognitive process) and illustrate it with Otto, a man with
Alzheimer's disease whose notebook does for him what biological memory does for others. They argue
that the notebook's entries are among Otto's dispositional beliefs, provided the resource is reliably
available, readily consulted, automatically endorsed and was endorsed in the past. They explicitly
leave consciousness out of the thesis. Grounds [extended-mind](../concepts/extended-mind.md).

Reference:
- Clark, A., & Chalmers, D. (1998). The extended mind. *Analysis*, *58*(1), 7–19. https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/58.1.7
