relevance (content): Poincaré's first-person account of how he discovered results in the theory of
Fuchsian functions. After a period of fruitless conscious work he set the problem aside to travel;
the decisive idea came to him, unprompted and with "perfect certainty," at the moment he stepped
onto an omnibus at Coutances, while he was talking of other things. Poincaré took such episodes as
evidence of prolonged *unconscious work* that delivers its result to consciousness ready-made. The
canonical anecdote behind [incubation](../concepts/incubation.md), and the main source on which Wallas built the incubation and
illumination stages.

Reference:
- Poincaré, H. (1913). Mathematical creation. In *The foundations of science* (G. B. Halsted, Trans.; pp. 383–394). The Science Press. (Original work published 1908)
