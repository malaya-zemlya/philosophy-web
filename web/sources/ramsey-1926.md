relevance (content): Ramsey's revision of the *Principia Mathematica* foundations. It introduces the
now-standard division of the paradoxes into *logical* contradictions, such as Russell's and
Burali-Forti's, which involve only logical and mathematical notions, and what Ramsey called
*epistemological* contradictions, now usually called *semantic*, such as the Liar, Berry's and
Grelling's, which involve notions such as meaning, naming or truth. He argued that only the first
group calls for a hierarchy of types in logic itself, and that the second arises from faulty ideas
about language. Cited in [grelling-nelson-paradox](../concepts/grelling-nelson-paradox.md).

Reference:
- Ramsey, F. P. (1926). The foundations of mathematics. *Proceedings of the London Mathematical
  Society* (Series 2), *25*, 338–384.
