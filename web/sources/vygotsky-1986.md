relevance (content): the classic developmental account of inner speech. Children first use speech
socially, to direct and be directed by others; they then talk aloud to themselves ("private speech")
to steer their own activity; and this self-directed speech is finally internalized, becoming
abbreviated, predicate-heavy inner speech. On this view inner speech is a tool of self-regulation
inherited from social communication, not merely thought's echo in words. Cited in
[cot-illuminates-inner-speech](../claims/cot-illuminates-inner-speech.md).

Reference:
- Vygotsky, L. S. (1986). *Thought and language* (A. Kozulin, Trans.). MIT Press. (Original work published 1934)
