relevance (content): the classic early statement of the Buddhist no-self view in dialogue form. Questioned by the Indo-Greek king Milinda, the monk Nāgasena argues that "Nāgasena" is a mere designation: just as a chariot is neither its pole, axle, wheels, nor frame, nor something over and above them, yet "chariot" is a usable name for the assembled parts, so the person is a conventional label for the aggregates and no self is found among or beyond them. Grounds the chariot simile in [no-self](../positions/no-self.md) and the Buddhist dissent recorded in [soul](../concepts/soul.md) and [soul](../questions/soul.md).

Reference:
- *The questions of King Milinda* (T. W. Rhys Davids, Trans.; Sacred Books of the East, Vols. 35–36). (1890–1894). Clarendon Press.
