relevance (content): the standard modern statement of the distinction between *weak* emergence (a
high-level phenomenon that is unexpected given the low-level facts but deducible from them in
principle) and *strong* emergence (one whose truths are not deducible even in principle from the
low-level truths). Chalmers argues that consciousness is the one clear candidate for strong
emergence. Grounds [emergence](../concepts/emergence.md).

Reference:
- Chalmers, D. J. (2006). Strong and weak emergence. In P. Clayton & P. Davies (Eds.), *The
  re-emergence of emergence: The emergentist hypothesis from science to religion* (pp. 244–254).
  Oxford University Press.
