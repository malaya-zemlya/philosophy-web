relevance (content): George Graham and Terence Horgan's redesign of the knowledge argument around
"Mary Mary", a scientist who also accepts a materialist theory of phenomenal content, and who on
release is surprised and delighted by her colour experiences. On pp. 76–77 they consider, and concede,
that she could predict in advance that she would be surprised; they call that predictability "beside
the point" and pose instead the challenge of explaining why her surprise is *rationally appropriate*
without treating it as an illusion or error. In footnote 7 they leave open whether "Mary Mary cannot
both know that she will be surprised in a certain way and be surprised in that way." Both points are
taken up in [surprise-predictable-yet-undergone](../claims/surprise-predictable-yet-undergone.md) and [llmary-predicted-surprise](../arguments/llmary-predicted-surprise.md).
Daniel Dennett replies to the paper in "What RoboMary knows" ([dennett-2005](dennett-2005.md)).

Reference:
- Graham, G., & Horgan, T. (2000). Mary Mary, quite contrary. *Philosophical Studies*, *99*(1),
  59–87. https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1018779425306
