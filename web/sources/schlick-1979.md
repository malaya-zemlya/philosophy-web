relevance (content): Schlick's three London lectures of 1932, published in English in the second volume of his *Philosophical Papers*. Their central thesis is that knowledge and communication capture only the *structure* (form) of what is experienced, never its *content* (quality): two speakers can agree on every structural relation among their experiences while the qualities themselves remain inexpressible. Grounds the contrast between communicable structure and incommunicable experience in [epistemic-access-to-experience](../questions/epistemic-access-to-experience.md).

Reference:
- Schlick, M. (1979). Form and content: An introduction to philosophical thinking. In H. L. Mulder & B. F. B. van de Velde-Schlick (Eds.), *Philosophical papers: Vol. II. (1925–1936)* (pp. 285–369). D. Reidel. (Original lectures delivered 1932)
