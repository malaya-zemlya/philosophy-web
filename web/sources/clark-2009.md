relevance (content): Andy Clark's argument that, although cognition may extend into the world, the
machinery of conscious experience is probably still in the head. Conscious experience seems to
depend on fast, high-bandwidth integration of information, and the loops that connect a brain to its
external resources are too slow and narrow to take part in it. The leading statement, by a founder
of the extended mind thesis, of [extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness](../claims/extended-cognition-not-extended-consciousness.md).

Reference:
- Clark, A. (2009). Spreading the joy? Why the machinery of consciousness is (probably) still in the head. *Mind*, *118*(472), 963–993. https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/fzp110
