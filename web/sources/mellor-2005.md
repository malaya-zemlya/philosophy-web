relevance (content): argues, mainly from fetal sheep physiology, that the mammalian fetus is never awake in utero. A set of neuroinhibitory factors (adenosine, the neurosteroids allopregnanolone and pregnanolone, prostaglandin D2, and a placental peptide inhibitor), together with warmth and cushioning, keep it in sleep-like states, so that it cannot be aware of noxious stimuli even after the neural pathways mature. Awareness begins only with the arousal at birth. Grounds the neuroinhibition thesis in [onset-of-consciousness](../questions/onset-of-consciousness.md); extended to the lamb and chick in [mellor-diesch-2007](mellor-diesch-2007.md).

Reference:
- Mellor, D. J., Diesch, T. J., Gunn, A. J., & Bennet, L. (2005). The importance of 'awareness' for understanding fetal pain. *Brain Research Reviews*, *49*(3), 455–471. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainresrev.2005.01.006
