relevance (content): among other things, a classic case for *cognitive phenomenology*, the view that
understanding has its own experiential character. Strawson's example contrasts a speaker of French with
a monolingual English speaker listening to the same French speech: the sounds are the same for both, but
there is something it is like for the first to understand them, which he calls *understanding-experience*.
Cited in [intermediate-level-theory](../positions/intermediate-level-theory.md) as an objection to confining consciousness to sound.

Reference:
- Strawson, G. (1994). *Mental reality*. MIT Press.
