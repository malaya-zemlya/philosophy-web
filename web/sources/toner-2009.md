relevance (content): the leading case for the corruptionist reading of Aquinas: since the human being is a composite of soul and body, a human person ceases to exist at death, and only the separated soul, which is not the person, persists until the resurrection. Grounds the corruptionist side of the survivalism debate recorded in [soul](../concepts/soul.md) and [soul](../questions/soul.md).

Reference:
- Toner, P. (2009). Personhood and death in St. Thomas Aquinas. *History of Philosophy Quarterly*, *26*(2), 121–138.
