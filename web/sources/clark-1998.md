relevance (content): Andy Clark's argument that public language is a cognitive tool that augments
the computational powers of a brain that is, at bottom, a pattern-completing associative engine.
Words let us hold, manipulate and reflect on thoughts in ways that would otherwise be unavailable,
much as pen and paper extend arithmetic. A precursor of the idea that serial verbal records change
what a fixed processor can compute ([cot-illuminates-inner-speech](../claims/cot-illuminates-inner-speech.md)). Not to be confused
with Austen Clark's *Sensory Qualities* ([clark-1993](clark-1993.md)).

Reference:
- Clark, A. (1998). Magic words: How language augments human computation. In P. Carruthers & J. Boucher (Eds.), *Language and thought: Interdisciplinary themes* (pp. 162–183). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511597909.011
