relevance (content): Eddington's Gifford Lectures, delivered in 1927, and an early precursor of
Russellian monism alongside [russell-1927](russell-1927.md). Physics, he argues, describes only "pointer
readings" and is silent on the *background* those readings are attached to (p. 252). In the one case of
his own brain he knows that background to be consciousness, and he concludes that "the stuff of the
world is mind-stuff," while adding that this mind-stuff is "something more general than our individual
conscious minds" (p. 276). Grounds the historical section of [russellian-monism](../positions/russellian-monism.md), where the
passage is the first experiential reading of the intrinsic nature that physics leaves blank.

Reference:
- Eddington, A. S. (1928). *The nature of the physical world*. Cambridge University Press.
