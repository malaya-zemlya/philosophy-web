relevance (content): the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* is the oldest surviving systematic text of classical Sāṃkhya. It sets out the dualism of pure, inactive, plural consciousnesses (*puruṣa*) and an active, unconscious primal matter (*prakṛti*) from which intellect, ego, mind, senses, and elements evolve, with arguments for the existence and plurality of *puruṣa* (verses 17–18) and liberation as isolation (*kaivalya*). Larson's study contains the Sanskrit in transliteration with an English translation. Grounds [samkhya](../positions/samkhya.md) and its place in [soul](../concepts/soul.md).

Reference:
- Larson, G. J. (1979). *Classical Sāṃkhya: An interpretation of its history and meaning* (2nd rev. ed.). Motilal Banarsidass.
