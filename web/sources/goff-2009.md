relevance (content): Goff's conceivability argument against subject-summing. Reading James's
"hundred feelings" passage as a claim about conceivability, he argues that one can coherently conceive
any group of subjects existing without any further subject, and hence a *micro-experiential zombie*
(a creature physically like us whose every micro-part is conscious but which has no macro-experience).
If that is possible, micro-experiential facts do not ground macro-experience, and constitutive
panpsychism inherits the very explanatory gap it was meant to close. Grounds the subject-summing
section of [combination-problem-panpsychism](../concepts/combination-problem-panpsychism.md).

Reference:
- Goff, P. (2009). Why panpsychism doesn't help us explain consciousness. *Dialectica*, *63*(3),
  289–311. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1746-8361.2009.01196.x
