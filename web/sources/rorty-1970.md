relevance (content): proposes that what makes thoughts and sensations paradigmatically mental is that first-person reports of them are treated as incorrigible, meaning no one is in a position to overrule them, and argues that this status is a social practice that could change with neuroscience. Grounds the incorrigibility conception of privileged access in [epistemic-access-to-experience](../questions/epistemic-access-to-experience.md).

Reference:
- Rorty, R. (1970). Incorrigibility as the mark of the mental. *The Journal of Philosophy*, *67*(12), 399–424. https://doi.org/10.2307/2024002
