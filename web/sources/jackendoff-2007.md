relevance (content): Jackendoff's later restatement of the intermediate-level theory (ch. 3). When we
experience language, the forms in awareness most closely mirror phonological structure (p. 81), not
the acoustic signal and not syntax or meaning; tip-of-the-tongue states, in which the meaning is present
but the sound is missing, show that meaning without phonology does not yield linguistic experience. He
notes the view's counterintuitive upshot: "How can the contents of consciousness consist of just a
string of sounds?" (p. 85). Grounds [intermediate-level-theory](../positions/intermediate-level-theory.md).

Reference:
- Jackendoff, R. (2007). *Language, consciousness, culture: Essays on mental structure*. MIT Press.
