relevance (content): the programmatic proposal to make AI self-reports trustworthy evidence about a
system's own states, cited by [ai-introspection](../questions/ai-introspection.md). Because current model self-reports are
driven largely by imitation of human text and by training incentives, they are poor evidence about
consciousness or moral status. The authors propose training models to answer many questions about
themselves whose answers are independently known, with the aim of instilling introspection-like
capacities that generalize, and then testing whether those capacities transfer to questions about
states relevant to moral status. They discuss how to evaluate such self-reports and the risks of the
approach, including training models to say what experimenters expect.

Reference:
- Perez, E., & Long, R. (2023). *Towards evaluating AI systems for moral status using self-reports* (arXiv:2311.08576). arXiv. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.08576

framing to preserve when citing:
- It is a **research proposal**, not an empirical result: it reports no finding that any model
  introspects or has morally relevant states.
- It treats self-reports as one **defeasible** line of evidence about moral status, to be validated,
  not as decisive.
