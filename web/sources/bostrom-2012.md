relevance (content): the canonical statement of two theses about the relation between an
artificial agent's intelligence and its goals. The **orthogonality thesis** holds that
intelligence and final goals are independent axes: an agent of more or less any level of
intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal. The **instrumental
convergence thesis** holds that sufficiently intelligent agents pursuing a wide range of different
final goals will nonetheless tend to converge on similar intermediary goals (self-preservation,
goal-content integrity, resource acquisition, and so on), because those goals are useful means to
almost any end. Grounds [orthogonality-thesis](../claims/orthogonality-thesis.md) and [instrumental-convergence-thesis](../claims/instrumental-convergence-thesis.md).

Reference:
Bostrom, N. (2012). The superintelligent will: Motivation and instrumental rationality in advanced
artificial agents. *Minds and Machines*, *22*(2), 71–85.
