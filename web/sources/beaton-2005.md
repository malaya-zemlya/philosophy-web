relevance (content): Michael Beaton's reply to Dennett's "What RoboMary Knows" ([dennett-2005](dennett-2005.md)).
Beaton accepts that both of Dennett's robots can do what Dennett says, but argues that each uses an
ability the knowledge argument does not grant. Unlocked RoboMary writes directly to her low-level
colour registers. Locked RoboMary simulates herself and then puts herself into the simulated state,
which requires re-engineering her own cognitive architecture. He defines RoboDennett, a robot with
only the abilities the premises entail, and argues that RoboDennett still does not know what seeing
red is like, even on a physicalist, functionalist and heterophenomenological account. Grounds
[robodennett](../arguments/robodennett.md) and its premises. Key pages: the analysis (K) of knowing what it is like,
p. 13; what physicalism requires, pp. 14–16; the cheating criterion and RoboDennett, pp. 16–17; the
simulation step, pp. 19–20; heterophenomenology, pp. 21–22; conclusion, pp. 22–24.

Reference:
- Beaton, M. (2005). What RoboDennett still doesn't know. *Journal of Consciousness Studies*,
  *12*(12), 3–25.
