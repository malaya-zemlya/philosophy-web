relevance (content): Frank Jackson's short restatement of the knowledge argument and his reply to Paul
Churchland's objections ([churchland-1985](churchland-1985.md)). Two passages matter to the web. On p. 291 he
treats Mary's reaction on release as evidence that she lacked knowledge: she "will learn what it is
like to see something red … This is rightly described as learning—she will not say 'ho, hum.'" On
p. 292 he sets aside what Mary could imagine: "Powers of imagination are not to the point …
Imagination is a faculty that those who lack knowledge need to fall back on." The first is the
inference from surprise to missing knowledge that [llmary-predicted-surprise](../arguments/llmary-predicted-surprise.md) contests.
Distinct from [jackson-1982](jackson-1982.md), where the Mary case first appears.

Reference:
- Jackson, F. (1986). What Mary didn't know. *The Journal of Philosophy*, *83*(5), 291–295.
  https://doi.org/10.2307/2026143
