[intentionality](../../web/concepts/intentionality.md) is the "aboutness" of mental states: a belief is about something, and a
desire is for something. The [problem of intentionality](../../web/questions/intentionality.md) asks how any
physical state can be about anything. The first part of this chapter sets out the leading answers.
Intentional content may derive from phenomenal consciousness, from a system's interpretability under
the intentional stance, or from biological function, as in teleosemantics. Swampman and Kripke's
rule-following argument test these answers.

The second part is devoted to John Searle's [chinese-room](../../web/concepts/chinese-room.md). A man who speaks no Chinese
follows a rulebook for manipulating Chinese symbols and produces fluent answers. Searle concludes that
running a program is not enough for understanding, so "strong AI" is false. The argument has the
largest literature of any thought experiment about artificial minds. Its main replies each appear
here, followed by Searle's rejoinder: the systems, robot, brain-simulator, connectionist,
other-minds and "many mansions" replies. The chapter closes with the luminous room parody and with the
extended mind thesis, which places some of a person's cognition outside the head.

The Turing test and the distinction between strong and weak AI, which give the debate its terms,
appear just before the room itself.
