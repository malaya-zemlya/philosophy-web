[functionalism](../../web/concepts/functionalism.md) holds that a mental state is defined by what it does: its causal role
among inputs, outputs and other states. If that is right, the same mind could be realised in very
different materials, which is the issue of [substrate-independence](../../web/questions/substrate-independence.md). Functionalism is the
working assumption behind most of cognitive science and most hopes for machine minds. This chapter
collects the thought experiments built to test it.

The China brain imagines the population of China simulating a brain's functional organisation, and
asks whether anything would feel anything. Replies to it lead to the problem of grain: at what level
of detail are functional roles individuated, and does the choice decide the verdict? The fading and
dancing qualia arguments run the other way, arguing that a gradual swap of neurons for functional
duplicates could not change experience without the subject noticing. Two later entries question
whether those arguments assume what they set out to prove.

The second half concerns the lookup-table mind, or "Blockhead", which produces the right behaviour by
retrieving stored answers. It is used to show that behaviour alone is not enough for a mind. The
entries that follow ask whether such a table could physically exist, and what implementing a
computation requires. The chapter ends with simulation: whether a simulated mind instantiates a mind
or only depicts one.
