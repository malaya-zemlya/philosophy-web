The [meta-problem-of-consciousness](../../web/concepts/meta-problem-of-consciousness.md) asks why we think and say that consciousness is hard
to explain. That question can be answered in functional terms, whatever the truth about
consciousness turns out to be. [illusionism](../../web/positions/illusionism.md) takes the answer to be the whole story: our
brains represent our own states as having phenomenal properties that nothing has. On this view the
hard problem gives way to the problem of explaining the illusion.

The entries test illusionism from several directions. One dilemma asks whether the illusion exists
before philosophical reflection or is produced by it. Another asks whether the mechanism behind the
illusion can include animals and infants without also including simple feedback systems. A third
argues that illusionism combined with representationalism spreads consciousness far beyond brains.

The chapter then takes up the opposite starting point, the claim that for experience the appearance
is the reality, so introspection cannot be wholly wrong. Against it stand the claims that qualia can
be inaccessible to their own subject or misjudged by them, the cases suggesting that qualia are
wholly relational, and the argument that a "real feel" beyond everything reportable would do no
work.
