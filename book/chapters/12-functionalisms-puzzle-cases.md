Functionalism, set out in its own chapter earlier in the book, says that a mind is defined by its
functional organisation. This chapter collects the thought experiments built to test that claim, each
describing a system with the right organisation, or the right behaviour, and asking whether it has a
mind.

The China brain imagines the population of China simulating a brain's functional organisation, and
asks whether anything would feel anything. The fading and dancing qualia arguments run the other way,
arguing that a gradual swap of neurons for functional duplicates could not change experience without
the subject noticing. Two later entries question whether those arguments assume what they set out to
prove.

The second part concerns the [lookup-table-mind](../../web/concepts/lookup-table-mind.md), or "Blockhead", which produces the right
behaviour by retrieving stored answers. It is used to show that behaviour alone is not enough for a
mind. The entries that follow ask whether such a table could physically exist, and whether intuitions
about an imaginary one deserve any weight. The chapter ends by returning to [grain](../../web/concepts/grain.md): the
China brain and Blockhead together pose a dilemma about the level at which functional roles are
individuated, and the replies to it ask whether the organisational level escapes the dilemma or
faces one of its own.
