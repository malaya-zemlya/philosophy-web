This chapter leaves consciousness aside and asks what an artificial intelligence far more capable
than any human would mean for us. It begins with [intelligence](../../web/concepts/intelligence.md) itself and with the "AI
effect", the tendency to stop calling a capability intelligence once a machine has it. It then
turns to [superintelligence](../../web/concepts/superintelligence.md) and to whether it is feasible. The intelligence explosion is
the hypothesis that a system able to improve itself would improve rapidly. Arguments for and
against it concern whether the brain leaves room for improvement, whether returns diminish, and
whether intelligence is a single quantity.

The central philosophical question is whether intelligence constrains goals. The orthogonality
thesis, defended here on Humean grounds, says that almost any level of intelligence is compatible
with almost any final goal. The opposing view holds that a sufficiently intelligent agent would
reflect on and revise its goals. Instrumental convergence adds that agents with very different goals
would pursue the same means, such as acquiring resources and avoiding shutdown.

These threads meet in the paperclip maximiser, the standard illustration of a misaligned
superintelligence, and in the objection that such a system could simply be turned off.
