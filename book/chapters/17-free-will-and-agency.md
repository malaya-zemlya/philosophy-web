Do we act freely, and can we be responsible for what we do, if every event has prior causes? This
chapter begins with the [question of free will](../../web/questions/free-will.md) and the three classical answers.
Hard determinism says that determinism is true and leaves no room for freedom. Compatibilism holds
that freedom and determinism are compatible. Libertarianism holds that freedom requires genuinely
open alternatives and that we have them. Agent causation, the libertarian idea that an agent can
cause an action without being caused to, faces the disappearing-agent objection.

Two concepts shape the modern debate: sourcehood, the idea that freedom requires being the true
source of one's action, and the Frankfurt cases, which suggest that responsibility does not require
the ability to have done otherwise. The chapter then treats responsibility directly, as a
forward-looking practice justified by its effects, and asks how far it rests on instinct.

The last part presents a hypothesis original to this web, and the objections to it. Using the
mathematics of category theory, it proposes that consciousness and free will are dual to each other,
the one taking in the world and the other acting on it.
