Several debates in this book depend on what we can know and how. This chapter collects the
epistemology they draw on, centred on the oldest skeptical challenge, the
[problem of the external world](../../web/questions/external-world-knowledge.md). Can we know that a world exists
beyond our experiences, given that every proof seems to presuppose it? The entries start with G. E.
Moore's proof ("here is one hand"), the charge that it begs the question, and what dialectical
cogency requires. They then present the main ways of resisting the skeptic: common-sense realism,
Wittgenstein's hinge propositions, transcendental arguments, retorsion (showing that the skeptic's
own position undermines itself) and David Deutsch's argument against solipsism.

The second part concerns the structure of justification. The Münchhausen trilemma says that every
attempt to justify a belief ends in infinite regress, circularity or an arbitrary stopping point.
Foundationalism, coherentism and infinitism each accept one option. The chapter ends with
fallibilism and epistemic externalism, which take some of the force out of the trilemma.
