### The parts of an entry

Each entry opens with a **headword** in bold, the short name under which it is indexed. A small mark
before the headword shows the entry's kind (see Symbols). When the headword abbreviates a longer
statement, the full statement follows in italics. For a claim this is the exact proposition, and for
an argument its conclusion. A short line under it gives the kind of entry, its status, and its source.
"After Chalmers" means that the idea comes from that author's work, and "editorial" means that it was
introduced in the making of this web. A *contested* claim is one that an argument in the book
attacks.

The body begins with a plain definition or statement and then turns to the details, objections and
cases. Many entries end with a shaded **In plain terms** box, a short restatement for a reader with no
philosophical training. A **See also** list maps the entry's nearest relations.

### Sub-entries

Under an argument, the claims that are its premises and conclusion are printed as indented
**sub-entries**, each labelled with its role, for example "premise 2" or "conclusion". Under a
position, the claims it is committed to are printed in the same way. A claim used by several arguments
is printed as an entry of its own.

### Cross-references

A name in small capitals is a cross-reference to another entry, and in the digital edition it is a
link. A reference in parentheses, "(see Hard problem)", points to an entry that treats the matter
more fully. A reference in italics gives an entry's full title rather than its headword. Citations
of published works link to the Sources.
