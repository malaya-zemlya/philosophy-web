[functionalism](../../web/concepts/functionalism.md) holds that a mental state is defined by what it does: its causal role
among inputs, outputs and other states. If that is right, the same mind could be realised in very
different materials, which is the issue of [substrate-independence](../../web/questions/substrate-independence.md). Functionalism is the
working assumption behind most of cognitive science and most hopes for machine minds. This chapter
sets out the view and the questions it must answer about itself; the thought experiments built to
test it are collected in a later chapter, "Functionalism's puzzle cases".

The first question is one of [grain](../../web/concepts/grain.md): at what level of detail are functional roles
individuated? Any physical system can be described as running many different functional
organisations, and the decomposition-indeterminacy argument holds that functionalism owes an account
of which one fixes the mind. The entries that follow ask whether a system's causal and counterfactual
structure supplies that account, and conclude that it yields non-triviality without a unique answer.

The chapter ends with two questions that lead on to the next. Functional description captures only
structure and dynamics, and a simulation of a mind may instance its formal organisation while only
depicting its causal workings. Whether a simulated agent could nonetheless have qualia of its own is
the last entry.
